## Approach

A broad introduction to the earlier MSX cartridge library, built around Konami's action games, Compile's shooters and a strong variety of puzzles. Penguin Adventure, Knightmare, The Maze of Galious, the Nemesis games, King's Valley, F-1 Spirit, Zanac and Eggerland Mystery provide the foundations.

The collection also makes room for less obvious discoveries: Guardic, Alpha Roid, Mopiranger, Skooter, J.P. Winkle, Mole Mole 2 and Rock'n Bolt. Familiar arcade conversions remain eligible when their MSX interpretations are worthwhile. There is no fixed game quota, price criterion or requirement to reproduce another system's list.

The catalogue count includes alternate dumps, regional releases, programs and other variants, not 952 distinct commercial games. Inclusion is editorial judgement informed by research, not a claim of hands-on testing or unanimous acclaim.

| Area | Selection intent |
|---|---|
| Shooting | Nemesis I/II/III, Salamander, Parodius, R-Type and Zanac anchor the selection. Knightmare, Guardic, Gulkave, Star Soldier, Super Laydock, Juno First and Pastfinder broaden the perspectives and mechanics. |
| Platforming and exploration | The Maze of Galious, Dragon Slayer IV, The Castle, Castle Excellent, The Goonies, H.E.R.O. and the Lode Runner games offer different longer challenges. Thexder adds transforming action. |
| Puzzles and mazes | Eggerland Mystery, King's Valley I/II, Soukoban, Flappy, Skooter, Mole Mole 2, Binary Land, C-So! and Zenji provide substantial problem-solving. Both Bomberman games and several unusual maze games add variety. |
| Arcade action | Penguin Adventure, Antarctic Adventure, Comic Bakery, Magical Tree, Pippols, Circus Charlie, Athletic Land and Mopiranger are characteristic short-session choices. Mr. Do's games and familiar Namco adaptations provide alternatives. |
| Isometric adventures | Batman, Knight Lore, Alien 8, Gun Fright and Night Shade retain different takes on exploration and spatial judgement. Their original awkwardness is part of the recommendation, not silently patched away. |
| Racing and sports | F-1 Spirit, Hyper Rally, Road Fighter and Hang-On cover different driving styles. Konami's sports, Track & Field I/II, Hyper Sports I/II/III, the Hole in One games and Robo Wres 2001 broaden the set without collecting every sports release. |

Some choices are qualified discoveries. Jet Set Willy has period design friction; Spelunker is unforgiving; several arcade conversions have choppy scrolling or compressed presentation. Pay Load is a more unusual, guide-friendly driving/logistics choice rather than an immediate arcade favourite. These earn places without being presented as universal essentials.

## Release decisions

- Prefer suitable English European/shared releases when available, followed by original Japanese releases. A Japanese region label alone does not establish whether the actual game is difficult to understand.
- **Nemesis** represents Gradius. **Nemesis 2** is the distinct MSX game also titled Gradius 2, not the arcade Gradius II. **Nemesis 3: The Eve of Destruction** represents the game also titled Gofer no Yabo: Episode II; it is not an arcade Gradius III port.
- Select **Zanac v2**, retaining that exact DAT choice rather than both versions. Zanac EX on MSX2 is outside this catalogue.
- **Track & Field 1/2** represent Hyper Olympic 1/2. **Konami's Football** represents Konami's Soccer, and **Konami's Billiards** represents Video Hustler. Their Japanese counterparts are not additional selections.
- **Penguin Adventure** represents Yume Tairiku Adventure; **Antarctic Adventure** represents Kekkyoku Nankyoku Dai Bouken. These are distinct games and both remain.
- The DAT supplies the selected **Knightmare**, **Nemesis 2**, **Parodius**, **Road Fighter**, **Twin Bee** and **Yie Ar Kung-Fu** cartridge entries with an **Alt** label. Pin the unnumbered Alt entries rather than dropping these games or inventing untagged filenames. The label is preserved; it is not treated as a chronological revision number.
- **Circus Charlie**, **Hyper Sports 2**, **Salamander** and **Sky Jaguar** use the DAT's official **Wii U Virtual Console** entries. These are explicit release choices, not names to apply to an unmatched older ROM. Ordinary cartridge entries take precedence where suitable ones are present.
- BIOS files, Game Master utilities, educational software, tape conversions, demos, betas, pirate releases and modern homebrew are outside this selection. No fan translations or gameplay hacks are silently substituted.

## Deliberate overlap

The MSX versions of H.E.R.O., Pitfall, Choplifter, Boulder Dash, Wonder Boy and the arcade conversions are considered individually. Shared names do not establish identical layouts, controls or content. Conversely, a familiar title does not guarantee that the MSX version is preferable to another platform's.

Both Castle games, both King's Valley games, both Bomberman games and both Lode Runner entries remain for different challenges. Flappy: Limited 85 supplies another puzzle set. The two Hole in One releases are retained as sporting alternatives rather than regional duplicates.

Knightmare's scrolling shooting and The Maze of Galious's exploration justify separate places. The third Knightmare game, Shalom, is held for language rather than included merely to complete a trilogy. Dragon Slayer IV is the MSX interpretation of the family-based exploration game associated with Legacy of the Wizard, not an interchangeable NES ROM.

## Language, keyboard and cartridge features

**The Maze of Galious** and **Dragon Slayer IV** are deliberate guide-friendly exploration selections. Their hidden rules and progression deserve outside references. Galious also requires keyboard functions and typed boss incantations: ordinary directional controls alone do not cover the intended interface.

Japanese imports are not assumed to be entirely language-free. Pay Load, The Stone of Wisdom and the more complex adventures may need instructions or menu references. Selection accepts that learning requirement without pretending the retail ROMs contain a fan translation.

Shalom, Golvellius, the Dragon Quest games, Black Onyx, the Hydlide RPGs and other more text-dependent adventures are held for deliberate language and progression decisions. Historical importance alone does not make an untranslated RPG a good default recommendation.

Keyboard access remains relevant even in a cartridge collection: starting, pausing, changing weapons or selecting items can require keys beyond the joystick buttons. Level editors, cassette saving and optional multiplayer are extras rather than assumed prerequisites for enjoying the supplied stages.

**Salamander's additional ending route involves Nemesis 2 in the second cartridge slot.** Both games are selected, but two filenames in a manifest do not configure that combination. The ordinary standalone game remains worthwhile; the combination is a separate presentation choice. Konami's SCC-equipped cartridges likewise deserve their intended sound rather than an assumption that every ROM uses only the base sound chip.

## Scope and omissions

This list follows **Microsoft - MSX**, not the separate MSX2 catalogue. Metal Gear, Vampire Killer, The Treasure of Usas, SD Snatcher and later MSX2 favourites are not omissions to fill with similarly named files. Disk and cassette libraries also need their own selection and media treatment.

Japanese mahjong, shogi, quizzes, adult adventures, programming tools and further sports variants are not collected comprehensively. The aim is a varied playing collection with room for discovery, not a complete history of every software category.
