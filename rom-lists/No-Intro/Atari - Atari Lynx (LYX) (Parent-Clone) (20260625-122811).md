## Approach

A generous introduction to a small, distinctive library: arcade conversions, ambitious scaling effects, inventive puzzles and games that give the Lynx its own identity. Chip's Challenge, Blue Lightning, Todd's Adventures in Slime World, Warbirds, RoadBlasters, S.T.U.N. Runner and Crystal Mines II are central attractions.

The selection leaves room for discoveries and imperfect experiments rather than imposing a fixed quota. It retains much of the original commercial catalogue, but does not turn every prototype, homebrew demo or historical curiosity into a recommendation. The reduction statistic includes the DAT's many later projects and alternate builds, not just original commercial games.

| Area | Selection intent |
|---|---|
| Arcade action | Strong interpretations of Toki, Rygar, Robotron 2084, Joust, Ms. Pac-Man, Rampart and Xybots sit alongside the exploration and shooting of Xenophobe. |
| Puzzles | Chip's Challenge, Crystal Mines II, Lemmings, Shanghai and Ishido offer substantial quieter play. Dinolympics, Super Skweek, Block Out, Robo-Squash and Bubble Trouble broaden the ideas on offer. |
| Platforming and exploration | Slime World, Scrapyard Dog, Switchblade II, Shadow of the Beast, Gordo 106 and Fat Bobby provide different styles. Dracula: The Undead adds a short adventure rather than another platformer. |
| Vehicles and flight | Blue Lightning and Warbirds offer different approaches to flying. Battle Wheels, Battlezone 2000, RoadBlasters, Checkered Flag, Hydra and S.T.U.N. Runner show the machine's appetite for vehicle games. |
| Sports and alternatives | Awesome Golf, Jimmy Connors' Tennis, California Games and Super Off-Road lead the sports choices. Baseball Heroes, Hockey, Malibu Bikini Volleyball and Tournament Cyberball supply alternatives; chess offers a slower option. |

Several inclusions are qualified. Batman Returns is punishing rather than an easy licensed favourite. A.P.B. needs patience with its driving controls; Steel Talons is an ambitious but rough flight experiment. Zarlor Mercenary's slow pace and collision frustrations temper its appeal. These earn exploration slots, without being presented as essentials or preferred versions across all platforms.

## Release decisions and overlap

- Select ordinary English World/USA/European commercial releases where available. The retail entries take precedence over betas, prototypes and later collection copies of the same games.
- **Ninja Gaiden** is the arcade beat-'em-up conversion, distinct from the NES action-platformer. Ninja Gaiden III is omitted as a less compelling Lynx adaptation.
- **Gauntlet: The Third Encounter** is retained as its own game, not treated as an interchangeable copy of the original arcade Gauntlet.
- **Super Asteroids, Missile Command** is one combined cartridge and one manifest entry. The comma in its DAT filename is intentional.
- **Crystal Mines II** uses the original commercial release. Buried Treasure is held as an overlapping expansion rather than adding another large puzzle campaign automatically.
- Cross-system ports remain eligible when they offer a worthwhile Lynx interpretation. Toki, Lemmings, Rampart and Shadow of the Beast are not discarded simply because another console also has them.
- BIOS images, production tests, unfinished releases and alternative builds are not playable selections. No fan patches are implied by the filenames.

## Later-release exceptions

Three completed releases are deliberate exceptions to the usual original-commercial focus:

- **Zaku** adds a distinctive, polished horizontal shooter. The ordinary World release is selected, not its beta. Its unlicensed DAT label does not make it a pirate copy of another game.
- **Cyber Virus** adds mission-based first-person action. Songbird's completed release earns a place beyond the original Atari-era catalogue; the DAT's spelling is retained.
- **Xump: The Final Run** adds compact route-planning puzzles. Select the Retroguru digital v1.2 release rather than also collecting the older Retroguru and Songbird variants.

These are individual editorial choices, not a decision to include every aftermarket release. Jam entries, demonstrations and unfinished projects remain outside the list. Eye of the Beholder and Raiden are held for a separate review of their later-release versions rather than silently selecting prototypes.

## Presentation and controls

Portrait-oriented games remain eligible, notably Klax and Gauntlet: The Third Encounter. Their orientation is part of the intended presentation, rather than a reason to exclude them. Robotron's arcade controls need a comfortable handheld mapping; its appeal is not a promise of identical twin-stick controls.

Battle Wheels, Warbirds, Slime World and other linked-play games are selected for their solo value as well. The list does not assume emulated ComLynx multiplayer. Original difficulty and awkwardness are not silently removed with hacks.

Pit-Fighter, Hard Drivin', Viking Child, Kung Food, Double Dragon and Power Factor are omitted as less persuasive uses of playing time. Neither technical ambition nor a familiar licence guarantees inclusion. The sports selection is representative rather than exhaustive, and casino software is not needed merely to complete the catalogue.

## Cartridge format

This manifest follows the **LYX DAT**, whose raw `.lyx` cartridge images cover the original commercial library. The installed LNX DAT is a different set of entries and does not contain most of those classics; it cannot be substituted just because its extension looks more familiar.

Keep the verified library and manifest faithful to this DAT. Deployment to an emulator expecting headered `.lnx` files requires a separate conversion with appropriate cartridge metadata, including orientation where relevant. Renaming `.lyx` to `.lnx` does not add that header. This is a curation list, not a claim that the existing LNX deployment mapping can copy these files unchanged.
