## Approach

A small history-and-discovery collection, balancing early PET games with later homebrew. The appeal is inventive play within character graphics and severe hardware limits. These are editorial invitations to explore, not claims that every historical curiosity competes with later arcade or console games.

| Area | Selections |
|---|---|
| Early action | Afo, Bomber, Breakout, Blockade Special, Buggy, Carrace II, Cosmic Fighter, Crazy Balloon, Space Invaders, Space War |
| Cursor discoveries | BOP, Cops, Dungeon, Aliens!, Bonzo!, Fire!, Miner, Ouranos!, Ambush! |
| Slower historical choices | Adventure, Artillery, Microchess 2.0, PET Super Baseball |
| Later homebrew | Cross Bomber, Cross Chase, Cross Horde, Cross Snake, Cross Trex, Shuriken, Stinger |

Dungeon is a particular discovery pick for early graphical dungeon exploration. Ouranos! and Artillery retain turn-based variety; Microchess and Adventure preserve two different strands of early computer gaming. Typing and turn-taking are accepted exceptions to the preference for immediate action.

## Release decisions

- One ordinary catalogued image per game. Exclude bad-dump entries and alternate dumps. The unmarked Space Invaders is selected; its `[b]` alternative and the bad-marked Star Wars entry are not.
- Use the latest catalogued full-size builds of the Cross-Lib games. Cross Chase uses its 2021 16K edition rather than the many ASCII Chase and Cross Chase development releases, tiny builds and light variants. Cross Snake uses v2.0; Stinger uses the later October 2023 entry.
- Retain the original Cursor issue numbers and exact title punctuation in filenames. They identify individual games, not entire magazine disks. Avoid filling the selection with every Cursor program simply because it is historical.
- Bomber and Cross Bomber are a deliberate old/new comparison. Other repeated gameplay ideas remain only where they offer useful historical or implementation variety, rather than retaining every clone.
- English-friendly action and readable games lead. Educational exercises, casino games, generic board-game duplicates and simulations with substantial setup are outside the selection. The `(en-ja)` Afo entry is retained as an action-game discovery rather than a reading-heavy import.
- The companion D64 list adds BioTerror! without repeating any PRG selection. No unlisted PET classics are silently substituted from another archive.

## Handheld considerations

PET games often use keyboard keys rather than a joystick, so individual mappings matter. BASIC/ROM version, RAM and keyboard layout can also affect compatibility; later 16K-labelled homebrew needs at least that much memory. These choices have not been play-tested on the Brick, and no single emulator preset is asserted to suit the entire list.

Some early games assume local turn-taking. Adventure and Microchess require more keyboard interaction than the action selections. Their inclusion expresses historical interest and worthwhile variety, not equal gamepad convenience.
