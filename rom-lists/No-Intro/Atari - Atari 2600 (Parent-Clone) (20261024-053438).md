## Approach

A broad collection of games worth playing, exploring or understanding, rather than an attempt to preserve every familiar cartridge. The 2600 is especially rewarding for short score-chasing sessions, and its strongest games need little resemblance to later consoles to remain enjoyable.

Pitfall I/II, River Raid, H.E.R.O., Adventure, Yars' Revenge, Seaquest, Frostbite, Keystone Kapers and Enduro provide the foundations. Pressure Cooker, Solar Fox, Turmoil, Laser Gates, Tunnel Runner, Mountain King and Frankenstein's Monster make room for less obvious discoveries. Rarity and cartridge prices do not determine inclusion.

| Area | Selection intent |
|---|---|
| Action and exploration | Pitfall's two adventures, H.E.R.O., Montezuma's Revenge, Adventure and Mountain King offer different kinds of traversal and discovery. Haunted House, Secret Quest and Riddle of the Sphinx broaden the adventure selection. |
| Shooting and arcade play | River Raid, Beamrider, Demon Attack, Megamania, Seaquest, Spider Fighter and Moonsweeper cover different shooting rhythms. Space Invaders, Missile Command, Battlezone, Centipede and Stargate represent strong arcade interpretations. |
| Mazes and unusual ideas | Ms. Pac-Man, Jr. Pac-Man, Alien, Lock 'n' Chase and Bank Heist provide different maze games. Entombed, Q-bert, Reactor, Pressure Cooker and Solar Fox add less conventional challenges. |
| Racing and sports | Enduro, Grand Prix, Pole Position and BMX Air Master lead the vehicles. Boxing, Tennis, Ice Hockey, Fishing Derby, Bowling and Pete Rose Baseball represent different sports without collecting every publisher's equivalent. |
| Paddle games | Kaboom!, Warlords, Circus Atari, Super Breakout, Demons to Diamonds, Night Driver and Tac-Scan retain an important part of the machine's identity. Their control requirements are deliberate, not overlooked. |
| More ambitious experiments | Solaris, StarMaster, Robot Tank and Space Shuttle add strategy, simulation and cockpit play. Their manuals and controls deserve attention; they are not interchangeable pick-up-and-shoot games. |

This is more generous than a list of essentials. Donkey Kong and several smaller arcade conversions remain for their recognisable play and period interpretations, without claiming arcade fidelity. Private Eye and some later action games are demanding discoveries. E.T. is a deliberately qualified adventure curiosity: its rules and pit navigation need explanation, and its notoriety is neither a recommendation nor an automatic veto.

## Release decisions

- Prefer English USA/NTSC commercial releases, including shared Japan/USA editions. Suitable revisions within that region take precedence over earlier copies. Regional clones and alternate labels are not extra games.
- **Cosmic Ark** uses USA Rev 1, **Demon Attack** USA Rev 1, and **Astroblast** USA v1.1. Untagged originals are not added beside them.
- **Klax** deliberately uses the European PAL release. Its USA entry is not labelled as a prototype in this DAT, but the NTSC version was not the original commercial release. This is an exception to the usual USA preference.
- **Stargate** represents the game also sold as Defender II. The alternative Defender II entry is omitted. The earlier Defender is also omitted in favour of this more compelling interpretation.
- **David Crane's Pitfall II: Lost Caverns** and the long-form Pitfall! title retain their exact DAT names. Title punctuation, aliases joined with `~`, and `.a26` extensions are intentional.
- **Dark Cavern** supplies the 2600 interpretation of the Night Stalker idea. **Taz** is the selected regional presentation rather than collecting its reskinned counterparts as additional games.
- Routine anthology copies, multicarts, alternate dumps, unfinished releases, pirate variants and modern homebrew are outside this selection. Excellent later projects can justify their own deliberate expansion rather than appearing automatically.

## Controls and presentation

The seven paddle-focused selections are included for their quality and importance. Digital paddle emulation is available in Stella 2014, but sensitivity and control feel matter, especially for Kaboom!. This is not a promise that a handheld D-pad reproduces a physical dial. **Astroblast** is separately eligible for ordinary joystick play; its original manual supports both controllers.

**Stargate** and **Spy Hunter** use inputs from a second joystick for additional functions. They remain worthwhile with a suitable mapping. **StarMaster** uses the console's TV-type switch for its map, while **Space Shuttle** makes extensive use of console switches. These deserve deliberate bindings and instructions rather than being mistaken for broken games.

**Crossbow** on the 2600 is a joystick game, so it remains eligible despite the arcade game's light-gun origins. **Shootin' Gallery** is also a joystick game, distinct from the light-gun prototype Shooting Arcade. Sentinel is held for a suitable light-gun arrangement. Omega Race's Booster Grip, Indy 500's driving controller, Star Raiders' keypad and Kid Vid-dependent games also need separate control or accessory decisions.

**Combat** and other games whose main attraction requires a second player are held for a multiplayer collection. The selected Warlords has computer opponents; a selection such as Boxing can likewise offer solo play. Optional multiplayer does not imply a working local multiplayer arrangement on the handheld.

Game Select, Reset and difficulty switches are part of the original interface. Manuals help identify worthwhile variants, and some adventures rely on information supplied outside the screen. PAL and NTSC editions also differ in timing and colours; they are not treated as arbitrary filename alternatives.

## Deliberate omissions

Ms. Pac-Man and Jr. Pac-Man supply stronger maze chasing than the original Pac-Man port. Donkey Kong Junior, Double Dragon and several weak late conversions are omitted rather than included for their licences. Chase the Chuck Wagon and Air Raid do not earn places merely because they are collectible.

SwordQuest's external contest material and opaque progression are a poor fit for this default playing collection. Raiders of the Lost Ark is held for a deliberate two-controller inventory mapping and guide-led adventure decision. Supercharger games, including Dragonstomper, Escape from the Mindmaster and Phaser Patrol, are held as a separate expansion so their loading and presentation receive proper attention.

Educational, programming, diagnostic and casino software are not collected comprehensively. The goal is a varied playing library, not a museum of every cartridge category. Strong 7800 versions remain separate games in a separate catalogue; none are silently substituted for these 2600 selections.
