## Approach

A broad introduction to Sega's earliest cartridge library, centred on short arcade games and inventive puzzles. Girl's Garden, Doki Doki Penguin Land, The Castle, Gulkave, H.E.R.O., Ninja Princess and Golgo 13 offer reasons to explore beyond historical curiosity.

There is no fixed quota or automatic exclusion of games also available on later systems. Some adaptations are markedly simpler, but can still offer worthwhile play. The catalogue count includes regional variants, computer programs and unofficial releases rather than 247 distinct commercial games.

| Area | Selection intent |
|---|---|
| Action and platforming | Girl's Garden, H.E.R.O., Pitfall II, Wonder Boy, Drol, Choplifter and Ninja Princess offer different styles. Dragon Wang and Shinnyushain Tooru-kun add more qualified fighting and office-action curiosities. |
| Puzzles and mazes | The Castle, Doki Doki Penguin Land, Lode Runner, Championship Lode Runner, Soukoban, C_So! and Rock n' Bolt provide substantial problem-solving. Pacar, Sindbad Mystery, Hustle Chumy and Guzzler broaden maze play. |
| Shooting | Gulkave, Star Force, Exerion, Orguss, Star Jacker and Sega-Galaga cover different shooting patterns. Golgo 13, Bank Panic, Borderline and Yamato add less conventional settings and aiming. |
| Racing | Monaco GP, GP World, Hang On II, Zippy Race and Safari Race represent different early driving styles. Zoom 909 adds a further vehicle-and-shooting experiment. |
| Sports and quieter alternatives | Champion Baseball, Boxing, Golf, Ice Hockey and Tennis provide representatives without collecting every Champion title. Hyper Sports adds event variety; Champion Billiards is a puzzle-oriented alternative, and Othello supplies board play. |

The selection is intentionally generous. Early games such as N-Sub, Space Slalom and Sega Flipper are modest discoveries rather than essentials. Congo Bongo and Zaxxon are interesting hardware-specific interpretations, not promises of arcade fidelity. Several racers and action games have blunt difficulty or awkward movement; their inclusion does not make them universally preferable to later versions.

## Release decisions

- Prefer original licensed Japanese or shared international releases. This is not a USA-first catalogue: most selected games never had a North American release.
- Use **Rev 1** for Champion Boxing, Champion Golf, Congo Bongo, Dragon Wang, Flicky, GP World, Pitfall II and Wonder Boy. Use **Rev 2** for Monaco GP and Star Jacker. Earlier copies are not additional selections.
- Japanese-region releases without a revision remain eligible even when an English-labelled unofficial variant exists. An English title screen is not by itself a reason to prefer an unlicensed conversion.
- **Guzzler** and **Q-bert** are deliberate Othello Multivision cartridge selections within this shared catalogue. They do not imply that the Multivision BIOS or its built-in board game belongs in the manifest.
- Keep exact DAT filenames, including **Girl's Garden**, **C_So!**, **Shinnyushain Tooru-kun** and the `.sg` extensions. Familiar alternative names are explanatory, not instructions to rename files.
- Exclude BIOS images, test cartridges, betas, unofficial regional variants, modern homebrew and duplicate logo variations. Unofficial conversions are not silently used as substitutes for licensed releases.

## Deliberate overlap

Flicky, Wonder Boy and Doki Doki Penguin Land remain worthwhile despite later versions. Ninja Princess also earns a place alongside the later Master System interpretation associated with The Ninja. The aim is not to remove every shared idea across Sega's systems.

The SG-1000 Pitfall II is Sega's adaptation, not a reason to assume identical layouts and progression to the Activision versions elsewhere. H.E.R.O., Choplifter, Bomb Jack and Elevator Action are considered as individual adaptations rather than automatic copies of another platform's recommendation.

Both Lode Runner and Championship Lode Runner remain for their different level sets and challenge. The Castle and Soukoban supply different puzzle structures rather than being interchangeable block-pushing games. Champion Billiards is not included simply to fill a conventional pool-game slot.

## Language and controls

The default is ordinary controller play. Japanese presentation is accepted for action, puzzles and sports when the main attraction remains accessible. That does not guarantee every rule or menu is self-explanatory: Chack'n Pop, C_So!, Rock n' Bolt, Soukoban and the sports games may benefit from translated instructions or a guide.

The Black Onyx is held for a deliberate Japanese-language RPG decision. Mahjong, shogi and pachinko are likewise outside this default selection rather than assumed to be understandable from their titles alone. Champion Kendou, Pro Wrestling and Soccer are omitted in favour of the selected sporting representatives.

The Castle's original cartridge includes extra RAM; it remains a standard-controller selection, not a keyboard program. Its cartridge behaviour must be represented correctly. Level editors and cassette-saving facilities in games such as Lode Runner and Soukoban are optional extras rather than prerequisites for playing their supplied stages.

Keyboard-dependent SC-3000 educational and BASIC software, music tools, the drawing program and SF-7000 disk software are outside the controller-focused selection. A shared DAT does not make those media and interfaces interchangeable with SG-1000 cartridges. No computer programs are added merely to make the catalogue look complete.

Taiwanese MSX conversions—including Knightmare, King's Valley and TwinBee—are held as a separate unofficial-library decision. Some such conversions have additional RAM requirements; their familiar names do not justify mixing them into the original licensed collection without explanation.
