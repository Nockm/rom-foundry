## Approach

A broad introduction to the CPC, centred on action, exploration and puzzles that justify revisiting this computer on a handheld. Familiar arcade conversions sit alongside distinctive CPC adventures and later technical showcases. Recommendations are editorial judgements, not a claim that these images have been play-tested on the Brick.

Elite and Driller are deliberate exceptions to the preference for straightforward controls: they offer experiences worth retaining even though keyboard access and reading are more significant. Text adventures, management-heavy games, flight-simulator catalogues and annual sports duplicates are otherwise outside this selection.

## Release decisions

- Use exact `.dsk` filenames from this TOSEC DAT. Prefer an untagged, English-friendly image where one is catalogued; multilingual editions are acceptable. Language is inferred from catalogue labels rather than verified in-game. Skweek is an explicit French-labelled action/puzzle exception.
- TOSEC describes disk images rather than certifying an unmodified retail ROM. Some selected images carry `[cr ...]` crack credits or `[t ...]` trainer tags. Retain these labels; a matching checksum establishes which catalogued image we have, not that it is an original retail master. Prefer non-trainer versions when alternatives exist, and use normal-play options where a trainer menu permits them. Entries marked as bad dumps or hacks are excluded.
- Renegade means the 1987 Imagine arcade conversion, not the unrelated 1985 Kuma game. Gauntlet means the 1986 US Gold conversion, not Micro Power's earlier namesake. Barbarian means Palace's 1987 fighting game, not Melbourne House's different game. Tetris uses Mirrorsoft's 1987 release.
- Get Dexter represents Crafton & Xunk once; Get Dexter 2 represents its sequel, rather than duplicating it under the French title. Sorcery+ represents the expanded Sorcery experience. Marble Madness uses Deluxe Edition rather than the Construction Set.
- Keep every disk in the chosen edition of Chip's Challenge, Lemmings, Megablasters, North & South, Shadow of the Beast, Turrican, Turrican II and Xyphoes Fantasy. Do not mix disks from different tagged editions. Xyphoes uses the three-disk edition; North & South retains its catalogued `[CPM Version]` pair.
- Alternate dumps are omitted unless needed to complete an edition. Selection does not expand into another DAT merely because a title is familiar. Cartridge-only releases, modern games absent from this disk catalogue and fan translations need separate, explicit choices.

## Handheld considerations

These are computer disks: launching, selecting joystick controls and some in-game actions can require keyboard input. Multi-disk games require disk changes; the filename-copy deployment does not create playlists or automate those changes. Prefer CPC 6128 mode for the collection, with the necessary emulator firmware and per-game mappings prepared separately.

Operation Wolf, Arkanoid and Marble Madness are included as CPC conversions, whose control options differ from their arcade cabinets. Lemmings and The Sentinel may be less comfortable than the more immediate action games. Inclusion preserves worthwhile variety rather than guaranteeing every game is equally convenient on a handheld.

The No-Intro CPC Misc starter remains a separate catalogue; this selection does not replace or duplicate its manifest.
