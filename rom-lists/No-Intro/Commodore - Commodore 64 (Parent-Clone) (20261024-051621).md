## Intent

A broad, approachable cartridge collection: early arcade games, distinctive C64 originals, and the ambitious cartridges from the C64 Games System era. Jumpman Junior, H.E.R.O., Gridrunner and Pang are anchors, with room for worthwhile discoveries around them.

The catalogue mixes cartridges with BIOS ROMs, utilities, educational software and some later releases. Its 394 entries count individual ROM files, not 394 different commercial games. The selection contains 104 cartridge files; two are compilations, so there are more playable games than launch entries.

Disk and tape classics such as Impossible Mission, Elite, Wizball, Paradroid, Maniac Mansion and Turrican belong in a complementary selection when suitable DATs are available. Their absence here is a catalogue boundary, not a judgement against them. Unofficial EasyFlash conversions are not substitutes for the original releases in this manifest.

## What earns a place

| Strand | Examples | Why keep it |
|---|---|---|
| Essential action | Jumpman Junior, H.E.R.O., Lode Runner, Pitfall II, Miner 2049er, Mountain King | Strong movement, exploration and repeatable challenges. These give the collection substance beyond arcade conversions. |
| Cartridge-era showcases | Pang, Toki, RoboCop 2, Navy SEALs, Batman, Battle Command | A useful tour of what later cartridge releases brought to the C64. Battle Command adds a very different kind of 3D action. |
| Larger adventures | Myth, Ninja Remix, Gateway to Apshai | Worth learning rather than expecting instant mastery. Their variety and ambition justify some extra patience. |
| Llamasoft | Gridrunner, Gridrunner II, Lazer Zone | Fast, distinctive shooting that gives the machine some of its own identity. |
| Arcade staples | Donkey Kong, Dig Dug, Frogger, Gyruss, Wizard of Wor, Q-bert, Tapper | Recognisable games with enough variety in pace and mechanics to earn separate places. |
| Shooting and rescue | Beamrider, River Raid, Choplifter!, Pastfinder, Zone Ranger, Save New York | A mixture of immediate score chasing, navigation and rescue objectives. |
| Puzzle and unusual action | Zenji, Oil's Well, Serpentine, Blueprint, Pipes, Jack Attack, Moondust | Discoveries that make browsing worthwhile, rather than filling every slot with another conventional shooter. |
| Smaller discoveries | Park Patrol, Toy Bizarre, Ducks Ahoy!, Peanut Butter Panic, Crisis Mountain, Sammy Lightfoot | Characterful games with a clear hook, even when their names are less familiar. |
| Driving and sport | BadLands, Pitstop, Pole Position, Leader Board, Decathlon | Different rhythms and skills, including a little button-bashing and slower play. |
| Compilations | C64 Games System pack; Power Play | Flimbo's Quest, Klax, MicroProse Soccer, Rick Dangerous and Stunt Car Racer make these meaningful additions. |

The list is deliberately generous with early games. Astroblitz, Lazarian, Rootin' Tootin', Mr. Cool and Mr. TNT are discoveries, not promises of equal standing with the strongest titles. Conversely, a famous licence does not guarantee a place: Double Dragon, Narco Police and After the War are left out.

Shadow of the Beast and RoboCop 3 stay as qualified inclusions. Technical ambition and atmosphere are part of their appeal; neither should be mistaken for an unqualified recommendation over the best games here. Rick Dangerous is another qualified choice: memorable, but built around learning traps through failure.

## Release and naming decisions

- Keep the DAT's `.crt` filenames exactly. These are cartridge containers, not filenames to rename to `.bin` or disk images.
- Prefer the ordinary USA/Europe cartridges where available. Omega Race and Wizard of Wor use those releases rather than the alternative MAX versions. Jupiter Lander, Kickman and Radar Rat Race retain their MAX-tagged releases because those are the relevant catalogue choices.
- **Gridrunner II - Attack of the Mutant Camels** is the game also known as **Matrix**. The separate US cartridge named simply **Attack of the Mutant Camels** is left out to avoid selecting that game twice. This is not the horizontally scrolling game about shooting giant camels.
- **Ninja Remix** represents the reworked Last Ninja 2 release, not a collection of the series. There is no invented Last Ninja 3 filename in this list.
- The C64 Games System compilation provides Fiendish Freddy, International Soccer, Flimbo's Quest and Klax. The standalone International Football cartridge is omitted to avoid repeating the football game under its alternative name.
- Power Play provides MicroProse Soccer, Rick Dangerous and Stunt Car Racer. Other compilations that mostly repeat selected standalone games are omitted.
- No prototypes, BIOS images, freezer cartridges, programming tools, trainers or modern unofficial conversions are selected. Later homebrew deserves its own considered selection rather than automatic inclusion because it appears in the DAT.

## Controls and deliberate holds

Cartridge loading removes a lot of disk handling, but does not make every C64 game controller-only. Expect to map start/function keys and occasional keyboard actions. Joystick port choice also matters; a game ignoring input is not automatically a bad ROM.

Gateway to Apshai, Ghostbusters, Myth and Ninja Remix deserve their instructions nearby. They remain worthwhile inclusions despite requiring more learning than Frogger. Decathlon's rapid directional input is a particular taste, not a requirement to complete the collection.

Robotron benefits from separate movement and firing directions. Spy Hunter needs access to its secondary actions; Chase H.Q. II likewise needs its extra action controls. Keep these games, but judge their handheld experience with an appropriate mapping. MAX-era paddle-oriented titles such as Clowns, LeMans and Pinball Spectacular are held rather than assuming a directional pad is an adequate substitute.

Ms. Pac-Man is a deliberate format hold: its DAT entry contains two differently sized `.crt` files. Selecting both would create duplicate launch entries, while choosing one without checking the containers would conceal an unresolved release decision. It merits revisiting, not rejection on quality grounds.

Keyboard-led dungeon crawling such as Maze Master, substantial simulators such as Space Shuttle, typing programs and school exercises are outside this handheld-focused selection. Super Sketch and other accessory-led software are also held. These choices keep browsing focused without implying that everything omitted is poor.

## Sources informing the choices

- [Steve Jarratt's contemporary cartridge recommendations in COMPUTE!, February 1992](https://www.commodore.ca/gallery/magazines/compute/Compute-136-02.pdf): a useful period perspective on the later cartridge releases, including Pang, RoboCop 2, Navy SEALs and Myth.
- [Commodore Format Archive: the C64 Games System releases](https://commodoreformatarchive.com/new-what-games-were-released-for-the-commodore-64-gs/): cartridge-specific context and the extra controls in Chase H.Q. II.
- [Jeff Minter's A History of Llamasoft](https://s3.amazonaws.com/lsshop/AHistoryofLlamasoft.pdf): the developer's explanation of Matrix's confusing US name.
- [Original Toki instructions](https://www.lemon64.com/doc/toki/615): the cartridge release and its intended operation.
- [VICE manual: machine-specific features](https://vice-emu.sourceforge.io/vice_7.html): cartridge containers, hardware types and emulator settings. Curation is not a claim that a particular NextUI setup has been tested.
