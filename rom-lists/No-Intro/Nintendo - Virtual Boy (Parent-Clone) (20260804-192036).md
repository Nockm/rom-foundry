## Editorial approach

A broad selection of worthwhile play and distinctive experiments, without treating a small catalogue as a reason to include everything. Wario Land and Jack Bros. are the strongest starting points. Other choices include qualified curiosities whose value depends more heavily on taste, controls or presentation. Research informed the selection; this is not a claim of hands-on testing of every title or compatibility with the target device.

| Area | Selections and rationale |
|---|---|
| Platforming and adventure | Virtual Boy Wario Land is the principal recommendation. Jack Bros. adds timed maze action. Dragon Hopper adds exploration and vertical platforming. |
| Arcade action | Mario Clash revisits compact arcade challenges; Red Alarm provides wireframe flight; Vertical Force offers shooting across depth layers. |
| Puzzles and pinball | Galactic Pinball, Panic Bomber and V-Tetris supply replayable alternatives. 3-D Tetris is retained as a spatial puzzle experiment, despite a less immediate learning curve. It is a different game from V-Tetris. |
| Sports | Mario's Tennis, Golf and Virtual Bowling cover three distinct activities. Virtual Bowling is the bowling representative rather than collecting both bowling games. |
| Unusual perspectives | Space Squash adds a depth-based bat-and-ball game. Teleroboxer adds first-person robot boxing with a distinctive control scheme, though its content is limited. |
| Historical discoveries | Innsmouth no Yakata offers a rough but unusual first-person horror/maze outing. Space Invaders: Virtual Collection is a qualified inclusion for its variations on a familiar arcade game. Zero Racers adds tunnel racing. |

These are editorial tiers in prose, not machine-readable ratings. Price, rarity and completing the catalogue were not inclusion criteria.

## Release choices

Prefer the ordinary English USA or shared English release where available. One release represents each regional game group. Japanese-region entries with explicit `(En)` tags, such as Virtual Bowling and V-Tetris, are eligible without translation patches.

The DAT includes **Dragon Hopper** and **Zero Racers** as `(Switch Online)` releases. Both are deliberately selected as official later releases, consistent with admitting official reissues elsewhere in this collection. Dragon Hopper is marketed as D-Hopper on the current service; the manifest retains the DAT's exact name. These are not the DAT's prototype entries.

Bound High remains deferred because this DAT labels its available versions as prototypes; its reputation alone does not silently override the present release policy. Other prototypes, demos, unofficial homebrew/aftermarket releases and fan translations are excluded. That is a scope decision, not a judgement that homebrew has no worthwhile games.

## Imports and omissions

- **Space Squash:** Japanese release, selected for accessible arcade play. Instructions may need a guide; do not assume that all text is English. Its lengthy campaign and lack of ordinary saving make session management worth checking.
- **Space Invaders: Virtual Collection:** Japanese release, selected for familiar arcade rules and its unusual presentation. Menus may need outside instructions; it is a lower-priority curiosity given the many other versions available.
- **Innsmouth no Yakata:** Japanese release, retained as a historical horror/maze curiosity. Navigation, instructions and presentation need review. No English patch or differently named reissue is substituted for this exact entry.
- **Nester's Funky Bowling:** omitted in favour of Virtual Bowling; it is a separate game, not a regional alias.
- **Virtual Fishing, Virtual League Baseball, Virtual Lab and Waterworld:** omitted on editorial grounds rather than filling every genre or release slot.
- **SD Gundam: Dimension War:** held for Japanese-language strategy review; rarity is not enough to earn a place.

## Handheld review

This is a system-wide curation list, not a promise that a flat-screen handheld reproduces the intended experience. Ordinary single-eye output loses stereoscopic depth. That is particularly relevant when judging distances in Space Squash, Mario's Tennis, Dragon Hopper, Red Alarm, Zero Racers and the spatial puzzles. Anaglyph output would require an appropriate viewing setup and is not assumed here.

The original controller has two D-pads. Teleroboxer and Space Squash warrant an explicit control-mapping trial on the Brick, as do other games using both directional inputs. Readability, palette, performance and save behaviour also need practical testing. No emulator configuration, firmware or performance claim is supplied by this manifest. A game can remain in the source-of-truth list while a later deployment-specific decision holds it back.

## Research inputs

The recommendation pool combines contemporary reviews, later retrospectives and specialist coverage. No fixed score threshold was applied.

- [Nintendo Life: Virtual Boy Wario Land review](https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/virtualboy/virtual_boy_wario_land) — an individual assessment of the central platforming recommendation.
- [Nintendo Life: Virtual Boy reviews](https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/virtualboy) — coverage of the broader catalogue, including imports and sports.
- [Planet Virtual Boy: review scores](https://www.virtual-boy.com/scores/) — historical and later critical perspectives; useful context rather than a single consensus score.
- [N-Europe: Space Squash](https://www.n-europe.com/features/all-virtual-boy-games-12-space-squash/) — firsthand discussion of play, controls and campaign limitations.
- [Nintendo Life: Teleroboxer review](https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/virtualboy/teleroboxer) — a qualified assessment of the boxing game's appeal and limited longevity.
- [Nintendo of America: official Virtual Boy announcement](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KKduF7Ux5dU) — confirms the later release of D-Hopper and Zero Racers.
- [Nintendo Life: D-Hopper review](https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/virtualboy/d-hopper) — assessment of the newly released adventure.
