## Intent

A broad Amiga collection built around its strongest action games, distinctive computer originals and worthwhile discoveries. Turrican II, Lemmings, Speedball 2, Sensible World of Soccer, Monkey Island and Cannon Fodder are anchors, with enough range to explore beyond the familiar names.

The list also makes room for adventures, strategy and dungeon crawling. A handheld collection need not mean arcade games only, but these games require a workable mouse mapping and sometimes additional keyboard controls. They are deliberate inclusions for slower sessions, not promises of effortless controller play.

The installed DAT preserves floppy disks as IPF images. Its 5,797 ROM entries include individual disks, regional releases, reissues and BIOS files. The selection contains 431 disk images belonging to 183 releases. That is not 431 different games: all disks of each selected release are retained, and the Cadaver release also contains its expansion.

## What earns a place

| Strand | Examples | Why keep it |
|---|---|---|
| Platform and action essentials | Turrican trilogy, Superfrog, Lionheart, Ruff 'n' Tumble, Fire & Ice, James Pond II, Prince of Persia | Strong movement and distinctive levels, with room for both immediate action and more deliberate exploration. |
| Arcade favourites | Rainbow Islands, Bubble Bobble, Parasol Stars, Rodland, Pang, Qwak, Dyna Blaster | Repeatable games with different scoring and movement systems. These form a particularly approachable part of the collection. |
| Shooting | Apidya, Battle Squadron, Hybris, SWIV, Silkworm, Disposable Hero, Z-Out, Banshee | Different perspectives and rhythms, including co-operative possibilities rather than a single style of shooter. |
| Cinematic and exploratory action | Another World, Flashback, Gods, Benefactor, Darkmere, Moonstone | Atmosphere, puzzles and exploration alongside combat. These deserve patience rather than arcade expectations. |
| Distinctive discoveries | Wizkid, Paradroid 90, Bubba 'n' Stix, Ugh!, P.P. Hammer, Virocop | Games with their own ideas that make browsing rewarding. |
| Driving | Lotus trilogy, Stunt Car Racer, Super Cars II, Jaguar XJ220, Crazy Cars 3, Vroom | A strong Amiga speciality, from accessible racing to more demanding vehicle handling. |
| Sport and pinball | Speedball 2, Sensible World of Soccer, Kick Off 2, Goal!, Pinball Dreams, Fantasies and Illusions | Several different approaches to competition; pinball also gives a clear use for handheld shoulder buttons. |
| Puzzle and planning | Lemmings, Lemmings 2, Push-Over, Troddlers, The Lost Vikings, Atomino, Puzznic | Problems to work through rather than simply faster reactions. |
| Graphic adventures | Monkey Island 1 and 2, Fate of Atlantis, Loom, Beneath a Steel Sky, Simon the Sorcerer | Important computer games worth retaining despite mouse input and disk swapping. |
| Strategy and management | Civilization, Colonization, The Settlers, Populous, Mega lo Mania, Syndicate, Dune II, SimCity | Longer sessions with meaningful decisions and a very different pace. |
| Dungeon and party adventures | Dungeon Master, Eye of the Beholder 1 and 2, Black Crypt, Captive, Hired Guns, Amberstar | Enough depth to represent another major part of the machine's appeal. |
| 3D and simulation discoveries | The Sentinel, Hunter, Virus, Carrier Command, Frontier, Zeewolf | Distinctive worlds and control systems, accepted with a greater learning commitment. |

The selection is generous, but inclusion does not imply equal standing. Shadow of the Beast stays for atmosphere and historical significance despite harsh play; Beast III adds a stronger puzzle-oriented alternative. Beast II is omitted. Rick Dangerous and its sequel remain qualified choices because their trap learning can be frustrating. Zool, Agony and Xenon 2 also stay as worthwhile but qualified inclusions, rather than being treated as unquestioned benchmarks.

Cannon Fodder 2 is retained for more missions, not because it improves every aspect of the original. The three Lotus games offer enough different formats to keep, while annual football revisions would add more repetition than discovery.

## Release choices

- Prefer English European releases and a PAL starting point. Use the available USA releases for Hybris, Zany Golf and the English Last Crusade graphic adventure. Region and machine configuration remain deployment choices.
- Prefer OCS/ECS versions where both those and AGA versions exist, keeping the collection centred on the classic Amiga experience. Do not duplicate the same game merely for enhanced graphics.
- The explicitly AGA-tagged selections are **Aladdin, Banshee, James Pond 3, Pinball Illusions, Super Stardust and Worms - The Director's Cut**. They are deliberate additions requiring a suitable machine configuration, rather than exceptions to be silently run as A500 games.
- **Alien Breed Special Edition 92** replaces the original Alien Breed in this list. Alien Breed II and Tower Assault remain separate games; Tower Assault uses the DAT's v1.1 release.
- **Project-X Special Edition '93** replaces the original. Its revised difficulty is the reason to choose it, rather than collecting both editions. It remains a demanding shooter.
- **Worms - The Director's Cut** represents Worms. The original is omitted to avoid a largely overlapping second entry.
- Keep **Sensible Soccer International Edition** for the focused match game and **Sensible World of Soccer '96-'97** for the broader club-career experience. Omit the intervening annual SWOS releases.
- Choose the **Cadaver + The Payoff v1.03** budget release: it provides the game and expansion together. Do not also select the older standalone game or separate expansion.
- **Ninja Remix on Amiga revisits the first Last Ninja**, unlike the C64 cartridge of the same name, which revisits Last Ninja 2. The title alone is not enough to identify which game it contains.
- Keep the ordinary retail disks where suitable, with individual revision choices such as Rodland v1.32, Kick Off 2 v1.6e, Frontier v1.05r4 and Captive v1.2. Gobliiins uses the English-capable compilation release available in the DAT.
- No demos, coverdisks, prototypes, marked bad dumps, trainer alternatives or BIOS images are selected. Required emulator firmware is separate from the games list.

## Controls and deliberate holds

Mouse-led games are retained because they contribute substantially to the collection, but they form a distinct kind of play. Cannon Fodder, Lemmings, The Settlers and graphic adventures need a usable pointer and mouse buttons. Dungeon crawlers add interface and movement demands; check a suitable mapping before committing to a long game.

These menu- and pointer-led games are a more promising fit than adventures centred on repeatedly typing commands. Parser-heavy adventures, elaborate flight simulators and dense keyboard-driven wargames are therefore held. Frontier, Hunter and Carrier Command are qualified exceptions for their distinctive experiences, not a precedent to include every simulation.

Japanese- or German-only adventures are not selected merely to fill gaps. In particular, the installed Ambermoon release is German; no fan translation is silently substituted. Modern unofficial ports and WHDLoad repackagings are also outside this DAT-based manifest.

Game manuals remain useful: copy protection may ask for information from the original documentation, while unusual controls can need additional keys. A verified disk image is evidence of identity, not a promise of frictionless play.

## Disk handling

Keep every selected `.ipf` file, including disks labelled as data, scenarios or other supporting content. A flat manifest preserves the set; it does not assemble a single launch entry or determine disk order.

PUAE documents IPF loading through a matching CAPSIMG library and multidisk selection through M3U playlists. IPFs are read-only, so saving also needs consideration. Deployment must supply the required firmware, arrange disk switching and confirm the installed emulator's IPF support. No builds, playlists or conversions are implied by this curation.

For disk-heavy adventures, a prepared hard-drive installation may eventually provide a more comfortable experience, but it is a separate deployment project. Preserve the DAT-matching originals rather than renaming files into another format or replacing them with unrelated repacks.
