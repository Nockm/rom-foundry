## Editorial approach

Broad quality and discovery rather than a short greatest-hits list. Familiar classics are joined by worthwhile licensed games, genre specialists, unusual handheld adaptations, approachable games and historical curiosities. No fixed game quota was used, and the size of other systems' lists was not a target.

Research supplied recommendation pools and independent perspectives, supplemented by editorial nominations. Not every selection has several external endorsements, and these are not claims of hands-on testing or device compatibility. Games with rough edges may earn a place for originality or a distinctive kind of play.

The list covers platforming, exploration and stealth; RPGs, farming and strategy; point-and-click-style adventures; puzzles, pinball and arcade games; racing, fighting, sports and card/board games. Discovery nominations include Wendy: Every Witch Way, Return of the Ninja, Power Quest, Warlocked, The Fish Files, Xtreme Sports and John Romero's Daikatana. A licence alone neither earns nor disqualifies a place.

The GBC Harry Potter games are selected as their own RPG adaptations, rather than assuming they resemble the GBA action games. Metal Gear Solid is the GBC game also known as Ghost Babel, not the PlayStation game. Handheld interpretations such as Resident Evil Gaiden, Alone in the Dark and Grand Theft Auto 2 are included partly for their unusual design or technical ambition, with compromises acknowledged.

## Scope and release choices

- This is the GBC DAT's catalogue, including dual-mode games labelled `GB Compatible`. Those files still belong to this manifest; they are not redirected into the monochrome Game Boy library simply because older hardware can run them.
- Prefer an English USA retail release, then a suitable World/European release; use newer numbered revisions within the chosen region where catalogued. Releases are pinned in the manifest, not chosen again during deployment according to what happens to be available.
- Retain the chosen releases' language tags and enhancement flags.
- Prototypes, betas, demos, pirate variants, unverified hacks, fan translations and modern aftermarket software are excluded by default.
- Prefer original English editions of text-heavy games, including Dragon Warrior, Lufia, Azure Dreams, Magi Nation and Metal Walker.
- Dragon Warrior I & II is a deliberate compilation choice. Dragon Warrior III is a separate game and has its own manifest entry. Do not confuse it with the similarly written I & II title.
- Dragon Warrior Monsters 2 uses Cobi's Journey as its representative; Tara's Adventure is omitted as an overlapping companion edition. Bomberman Max likewise uses Blue Champion rather than retaining both paired releases. Robopon uses Sun Version.
- Pokemon Gold, Silver and Crystal are all retained as an explicit version-choice exception, consistent with keeping Red and Blue in the GB selection. They overlap substantially; a later tighter collection could retain Crystal alone. Crystal uses the ordinary English Rev 1 cartridge release.
- Regional aliases have one representative: the USA Marvin Strikes Back! entry represents Looney Tunes Collector: Martian Revenge; V-Rally uses the USA Edition 99 release; Donald Duck uses Goin' Quackers; and Asterix: Search for Dogmatix uses the English-containing Sur la Trace D'Idefix entry.
- John Romero's Daikatana uses the European `(En,Fr,It)` release, not the different European language set without English. Other European selections, including The Fish Files and Moomin's Tale, are retained where appropriate.

## Overlap with other systems

Cross-system duplication is not removed automatically. The following are intentional choices:

- **Link's Awakening DX** adds the colour version to this collection while the approved original monochrome release remains on the GB list. Neither manifest was silently changed to replace the other.
- **R-Type DX** retains the colour package even though the GB list includes R-Type and R-Type II.
- **Donkey Kong GB: Dinky Kong & Dixie Kong** is the Japanese colour edition of Donkey Kong Land III. The monochrome GB version remains selected separately. Donkey Kong Country is a different GBC adaptation and is also included.
- **Wario Land II** is selected in its GBC edition; the present GB manifest does not include the monochrome edition. Wario Land 3 is a substantial separate game.
- Colour editions and ports such as Tetris DX, Super Mario Bros. Deluxe, Looney Tunes, Crystalis and Shadowgate Classic remain eligible even when the source platform may have a corresponding selection.
- Legend of the River King 2 is selected here. The first game remains represented by the current GB manifest; the GBC colour edition of that first game is not added as another duplicate.

These are editorial decisions for broad exploration, not a permanent rule against later deduplication.

## Imports, accessories and practical holds

Three Japanese-only releases are deliberately selected:

| Game | Accessibility consideration |
|---|---|
| Donkey Kong GB: Dinky Kong & Dixie Kong | Platforming is the main draw; Japanese presentation may remain. |
| Little Magic | Learn the puzzle rules and controls; menus and instructions are not assumed translated. |
| Guruguru Garakutas | A puzzle discovery whose rules and Japanese instructions may require a guide. |

No patched translation is substituted for these ROMs. Text-heavy untranslated titles such as the second Pokemon card game, Survival Kids 2, Grandia: Parallel Trippers and Sakura Taisen GB are deferred for an explicit English-release/translation review.

Kirby: Tilt 'n' Tumble is deferred until motion controls are verified for the actual device and emulator. Chee-Chai Alien and games dependent on specialist hardware are likewise outside the present selection. Mobile Golf is held pending review of language and peripheral-dependent features.

Pokemon Pinball and The Little Mermaid II: Pinball Frenzy retain their catalogued rumble releases. Rumble is an enhancement, not assumed device functionality. RTC behaviour should be checked for Pokemon, Robopon and relevant simulation games; IR, link trades, printer support and accessory features are not assumed to work. Collecting or progression goals dependent on trading may need separate consideration.

Shantae and Wendy are selected for their ordinary GBC gameplay; GBA-detection extras are optional.

## Research inputs

Lists and community recommendations supplied leads, not a rating threshold or proof of consensus. Mixed GB/GBC articles were filtered against the actual GBC DAT. Collectible value and cartridge rarity were not inclusion criteria.

- [Nintendo Life: 50 Best Game Boy Color Games](https://www.nintendolife.com/guides/50-best-game-boy-color-gbc-games-of-all-time) — signature games and a broad cross-check.
- [GameSpot: Best Game Boy Color Games](https://www.gamespot.com/gallery/best-game-boy-color-games/2900-3949/) — another editorial perspective on established recommendations.
- [Racketboy: Best Undiscovered Game Boy & Game Boy Color Games](https://racketboy.com/retro/best-undiscovered-gameboy-game-boy-color-games) — genre and discovery leads.
- [Racketboy forum: Gameboy Color Hidden Gems](https://racketboy.com/forum/viewtopic.php?f=2&t=52520) — supplementary community leads for adventure and strategy games, not independent verification of every claim.
