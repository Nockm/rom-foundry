## Approach

A compact collection centred on the games that give the 32X a reason to exist alongside the Mega Drive: convincing arcade conversions, early polygonal experiments and a few distinctive exclusives. The catalogue's size is not a reason to include every cartridge.

Virtua Racing Deluxe, Virtua Fighter, After Burner Complete, Space Harrier and Stellar Assault provide the strongest arcade foundation. Blackthorne, Kolibri, Knuckles' Chaotix and Tempo broaden the character of the set. Familiar sports and fighting games remain eligible when they offer worthwhile play rather than merely another logo on the box.

The reduction statistic includes regional releases and many beta builds, not 227 distinct commercial games. Selection is editorial judgement informed by reviews; neither rarity nor a rigid score threshold determines inclusion.

| Area | Selection intent |
|---|---|
| Arcade spectacle | Virtua Racing Deluxe's additional cars and tracks earn it a place beyond the Mega Drive conversion. Virtua Fighter, After Burner Complete and Space Harrier represent different arcade strengths. |
| Space and vehicle combat | Stellar Assault supplies free-flight space battles; Star Wars Arcade adds a different mission structure and familiar setting. Metal Head offers a rough but distinctive mech experiment. |
| Platforming and exploration | Blackthorne, Pitfall: The Mayan Adventure and Tempo offer contrasting movement and presentation. Knuckles' Chaotix supplies an unusual partnership mechanic rather than another conventional Sonic sequel. |
| Unusual shooting | Kolibri mixes hummingbird flight, shooting and exploration. Zaxxon's Motherbase 2000 earns a discovery slot for taking over other ships. Darxide is a more qualified technical curiosity. |
| Fighting and sports | Mortal Kombat II, WWF WrestleMania: The Arcade Game and Toughman Contest supply different kinds of combat. NBA Jam TE, 36 Great Holes, NFL Quarterback Club and World Series Baseball provide sporting variety. |

## Qualified inclusions

**Knuckles' Chaotix** is deliberately divisive: its tethered partners, momentum and presentation make it worth exploring, but it should not be approached as Sonic 4. The elastic connection can frustrate as readily as it produces an interesting idea.

**Kolibri** is more than a conventional scrolling shooter. Its movement, exploration and obscure objectives are part of the appeal and the friction; outside instructions may help. **Motherbase 2000** likewise has an inventive ship-capture mechanic alongside awkward depth judgement and slowdown.

**Darxide** remains for its textured 3D asteroid combat and ambitious presentation, with strict time limits and uneven play clearly accepted. Its collectibility does not make it an essential. **Doom** is retained as a historically interesting console interpretation with recognisable underlying play, despite its reduced content, presentation and compromised music. It is not the preferred way to play Doom generally.

**World Series Baseball** is a qualified sports choice with modest changes over its Mega Drive counterpart. **Metal Head**, **Star Wars Arcade** and **Tempo** also have limitations in pace, repetition or depth. They earn places for their individual appeal rather than a claim that the whole shortlist consists of masterpieces.

## Release choices and overlap

- Prefer suitable English USA/shared Japan-USA releases, followed by World or European editions. A Japan region tag does not imply a language barrier when the DAT explicitly includes English.
- **Stellar Assault (USA, Europe)** is the canonical filename selected here for the game commonly recommended as **Shadow Squadron**. These are not two separate selections.
- **Zaxxon's Motherbase 2000** represents the regional game also listed as Motherbase. Earlier beta entries titled Zaxxon's Motherbase are not additional finished games.
- **Darxide** uses the multilingual European retail release. There is no silent substitution of an NTSC conversion or unofficial patch.
- **Blackthorne**, **Pitfall**, **Mortal Kombat II**, **NBA Jam TE** and the sports games remain eligible despite versions on other systems. Their 32X editions are considered individually; automatic cross-system deduplication would remove worthwhile alternatives along with redundant ones.
- Ordinary retail releases only: exclude BIOS images, development programs, auto-demos, samples, betas, prototypes and unofficial ports. Doom RR beta entries do not replace retail Doom.
- Knuckles' Chaotix is not represented by an unfinished Sonic-related prototype, and modern hacks are not silently used to repair or expand the selected games.

## Deliberate omissions and scope

BC Racers, Motocross Championship, Brutal: Above the Claw, Cosmic Carnage and T-MEK are omitted as less compelling uses of playing time. Spider-Man: Web of Fire does not earn a place simply for its rarity and late release. Primal Rage is held in favour of the selected fighters.

FIFA Soccer 96's early 3D experiment does not outweigh its camera and play compromises here. RBI Baseball '95 is omitted in favour of World Series Baseball, and WWF Raw in favour of WrestleMania: The Arcade Game. The latter is an arcade-style fighting interpretation, not a substitute for a wrestling simulation. Star Trek's bridge simulator is also outside this default shortlist.

Sangokushi IV is held for a deliberate Japanese-language strategy decision. Soulstar X, X-Men, Virtua Hamster and other unreleased projects are not promoted into the finished-game selection. Modern demonstration ports and utilities are outside scope.

This is the **cartridge** catalogue. Sega CD 32X releases such as Night Trap and Corpse Killer require a different media workflow and are not missing cartridge selections. Six-button mappings are worthwhile for the fighters and Doom; the list does not assume three-button controls are equivalent in every game.
