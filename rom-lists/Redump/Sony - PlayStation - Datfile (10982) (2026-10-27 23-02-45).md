## Intent

A selective PlayStation shelf to follow by hand, rather than another automated build target. Short-session arcade games, platformers and puzzles sit alongside a smaller selection of longer adventures. These are editorial recommendations; inclusion does not establish performance or control comfort on the Brick.

The percentage compares disc entries, including the catalogue's regional variants, revisions and demos. It is not a storage saving or a count of unique games across the whole platform.

For a first handful, start with **Castlevania: Symphony of the Night, Klonoa, Tekken 3, R4, R-Type Delta, Silent Bomber, Hot Shots Golf 2, Mr. Driller, Tony Hawk's Pro Skater 2** and **Final Fantasy Tactics**. The wider checklist offers alternatives rather than an instruction to install everything.

## Release decisions

- Prefer English North American retail releases, normally the latest catalogued ordinary revision. Keep exact Redump spellings: for example `G. Darius`, `R4 - Ridge Racer Type 4` and `USA, Canada`. A familiar informal filename is not evidence of a particular disc revision.
- Wipeout 3: Special Edition and Vib-Ribbon deliberately use English-capable European releases. Wipeout's expanded track selection earns the exception; retain its PAL identity rather than treating it as the US game. Gradius Gaiden uses the Japanese Rev 1 release, an action import that does not rely heavily on reading.
- Gran Turismo and Gran Turismo 2 remain distinct experiences. GT2 includes both Arcade Mode Rev 1 and Simulation Mode Rev 2, the respective revisions catalogued here; these discs serve different modes. R4 and Ridge Racer Revolution likewise offer different styles of progression.
- Keep every selected disc of each multi-disc adventure. Resident Evil 2 includes both characters' discs. Resident Evil uses the ordinary Director's Cut rather than its separate Dual Shock edition; Resident Evil 2 uses its Dual Shock edition.
- Crash 2 and Warped, Spyro 2, Mega Man Legends 2, Dino Crisis 2 and Bushido Blade 2 provide representative series coverage without requiring every predecessor. Rayman and Xenogears remain despite their difficulty or unevenness because they offer distinctive experiences.
- Final Fantasy IV–VI and Chrono Trigger are left to the cartridge selections rather than repeated as PlayStation conversions. Already having Final Fantasy VI as a PBP does not make it a poor game; this is a choice about which version earns space in this particular collection.
- Ape Escape is outside this selection because its required dual-analogue control scheme deserves a separate hardware decision. Lightgun, specialist-controller and multiplayer-dependent showcases are also outside scope. Rhythm games remain, but their timing needs practical checking on the chosen setup.
- No fan translations, hacks, demos, bonus videos or making-of discs are silently substituted. Additional playable bonus discs can be considered separately.

## Files and deployment

This file is the source of truth for the manual shortlist. It deliberately has no companion copy manifest: the cartridge workflow cannot assemble disc tracks, convert images or prepare multi-disc launches.

Choose playable files manually and preserve each complete disc. CHD/PBP filenames do not prove that their contents match these Redump entries; the DAT describes original CUE/BIN layouts. Multi-disc games need a launch arrangement supported by the emulator, and protected releases may need matching subchannel support. Keep these preparation steps separate from the editorial selection.

Vib-Ribbon is selected for its supplied music; importing personal audio CDs is outside this list. No space estimate is implied by the game count.
