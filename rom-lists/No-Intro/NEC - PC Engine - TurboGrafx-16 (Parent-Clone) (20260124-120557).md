## Approach

A generous introduction to the HuCard library: its exceptional shooters, distinctive action games and puzzles, with enough adventures, racing and sports to make it useful beyond a single genre. Familiar Western releases and worthwhile Japanese imports share the list. Rarity alone is not a reason for inclusion, and there is no fixed game quota.

Bonk, Bomberman, R-Type, Blazing Lazers, Soldier Blade, Devil's Crush and Neutopia provide the foundations. Gomola Speed, Gekisha Boy, Parasol Stars, Final Match Tennis, Magical Chase and Silent Debuggers help show what makes this machine worth exploring in its own right.

| Area | Selection intent |
|---|---|
| Shooting | Preserve the strong Soldier games, Hudson and Compile collaborations, arcade conversions and distinctive imports. Coryoon, Hana Taaka Daka!, Magical Chase and Detana!! TwinBee bring a lighter presentation alongside Tatsujin, ImageFight and Kyuukyoku Tiger. |
| Platforming and action | Keep all three Bonk adventures, Ninja Spirit, Splatterhouse, the Legendary Axe games and Jackie Chan. Add regional discoveries such as Aoi Blink, Dragon Egg!, Momotarou Katsugeki and Genji Tsuushin Agedama. |
| Puzzles and unusual ideas | Gomola Speed, Chew-Man-Fu, Cratermaze, Boxyboy, Puzzle Boy, Puzznic and Timeball offer different kinds of spatial problem-solving. Alien Crush, Devil's Crush and Time Cruise provide three distinct pinball choices. |
| Adventures and strategy | English releases of Neutopia I/II, Cadash, Dungeon Explorer, Military Madness and Order of the Griffon make longer games accessible. Japanese action-adventures are considered separately rather than assumed to be language-free. |
| Racing and sports | OutRun, Victory Run, Final Lap Twin and the Moto Roader games cover different racing styles. Final Match Tennis, World Court Tennis, Power Golf, boxing, wrestling and unusual team sports provide variety without collecting every annual edition. |

Some choices are discoveries or historical curiosities rather than essentials. Keith Courage, Double Dungeons and several compressed arcade ports retain their rough edges. Power Drift and After Burner II are interesting interpretations rather than preferred versions across every system. Toilet Kids earns a curiosity slot for its unusual shooter presentation; its crude toilet humour will not suit everyone.

## Release decisions

- Prefer ordinary USA English releases where available, then suitable Japanese retail releases. Revisions are selected within that preference rather than collecting every regional alternative. Betas, prototypes, demonstrations, unofficial releases and routine reissues are excluded.
- **R-Type** uses the complete USA cartridge. The Japanese R-Type I and II cards divide the original game; they are not two additional sequels to collect here.
- **Blazing Lazers** represents Gunhed; **Military Madness** represents Nectaris; **Air Zonk** represents PC Denjin. Japanese titles are not added again merely to collect their alternate names.
- **Chew-Man-Fu** represents Be Ball, **Cratermaze** the corresponding Doraemon maze game, **Psychosis** Paranoia, **Somer Assault** Mesopotamia, and **Timeball** Blodia. The selected Western J.J. & Jeff presentation also stands in for its regional counterpart rather than adding both.
- **Bomberman**, **Bomberman '93** and **Bomberman '94** remain separate selections for their progression and play variety. The Japanese retail Bomberman '94 is selected rather than competition variants.
- **Darius Plus** is the HuCard entry explicitly tagged SG Enhanced in this DAT. SuperGrafx-only games have their own catalogue and are outside this list.

## Deliberate overlap

Ports are not automatically removed because another console also has them. R-Type, Gradius, Salamander, Parodius, OutRun, Shinobi and Street Fighter II' help represent this hardware's strengths and compromises. Their presence is not a claim that every port is the best available version.

Dragon's Curse and New Adventure Island are different games despite their confusing regional naming. The former belongs to the Wonder Boy III: The Dragon's Trap lineage; the latter is a separate Adventure Island platformer. Genpei Toumaden and Samurai-Ghost are likewise separate games, not two names for the same selection.

World Court Tennis retains its adventure-mode novelty alongside the more focused Final Match Tennis. Battle Lode Runner and Lode Runner: Lost Labyrinth offer different treatments of the underlying idea. Sequel coverage follows worthwhile differences rather than a rule to include every numbered entry.

## Language and scope

Japanese action games and puzzles are eligible when their appeal survives the language barrier, but that does not make every menu or rule self-explanatory. Valkyrie no Densetsu has dialogue and shop decisions; Tower of Druaga has opaque progression rules. These are deliberate guide-friendly selections. Japanese sports games such as Fire Pro Wrestling 3, Dodge Danpei and the Kunio games may also need menu or rules references.

Text-heavy untranslated RPGs and adventures, including Cyber Knight, Jaseiken Necromancer, Benkei Gaiden, Out Live and the Momotarou Densetsu RPGs, are held for a separate language decision. Momotarou Katsugeki is an action game and remains eligible. Fan translations are not silently substituted for the listed retail ROMs.

Mahjong, quizzes and gambling-focused games are not collected comprehensively. Weaker curiosities such as Deep Blue, Energy and Rastan Saga II do not earn places simply because they are associated with the platform.

This is a HuCard selection. CD releases such as Rondo of Blood, Ys Book I & II, Gate of Thunder, Lords of Thunder and Spriggan belong to a separate catalogue. Their absence here is a format boundary, not a judgement against them. Multiplayer recommendations describe the games' appeal without assuming that a handheld provides the original multiplayer setup.
