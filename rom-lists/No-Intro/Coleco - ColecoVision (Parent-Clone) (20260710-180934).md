## Approach

A broad introduction to the commercial library, giving its arcade conversions plenty of space without reducing the machine to Donkey Kong and Zaxxon. Lady Bug, Pepper II, Jumpman Junior, Mr. Do!, Frenzy and Root Beer Tapper are central attractions. Oil's Well, Zenji, Rock n' Bolt, Rolloverture and Fortune Builder help make the collection more distinctive.

Quality, different ideas and worthwhile interpretations matter more than rarity or a fixed game count. This is deliberately more generous than an essentials list. The catalogue count includes regional copies, prototypes and non-game entries rather than 201 distinct commercial games.

| Area | Selection intent |
|---|---|
| Arcade action | Both Donkey Kong games, BurgerTime, Mr. Do! and its Castle companion, Popeye, Bump 'n' Jump and Root Beer Tapper offer contrasting arcade loops. Antarctic Adventure adds straightforward, cheerful course-running. |
| Mazes and puzzles | Lady Bug, Pepper II, Mouse Trap, Oil's Well, Zenji, Q-bert and Q-bert's Qubes provide different spatial challenges. Rolloverture, Rock n' Bolt, Linking Logic and Logic Levels supply less familiar puzzle ideas. |
| Shooting | Frenzy, Gyruss, Beamrider, River Raid, Moonsweeper and Cosmic Avenger cover different shooting styles. Zaxxon, Buck Rogers, Space Fury, Star Trek and Star Wars add different perspectives and vehicle combat. |
| Platforming and exploration | Jumpman Junior, Miner 2049er, H.E.R.O., Montezuma's Revenge, Pitfall I/II and Mountain King lead the selection. Cabbage Patch Kids, Smurf Rescue, BC's Quest for Tires and Sammy Lightfoot bring lighter or more unusual alternatives. |
| Adventures and strategy | Gateway to Apshai and Alcazar offer dungeon exploration. Fortune Builder, WarGames, War Room and 2010 broaden the set beyond reflex games. The Dam Busters is a more demanding simulation curiosity. |
| Sports and playful learning | Pitstop, Skiing and Tournament Tennis offer representative sports. Monkey Academy, Fraction Fever, Amazing Bumpman and Dr. Seuss's puzzle game earn places for their play ideas rather than a commitment to collect educational software. |

Some inclusions are qualified discoveries rather than essentials. Blockade Runner and Aquattack have unusual perspectives and controls; Sewer Sam and Slurpy are eccentric experiments. Gateway to Apshai's dungeon action has rough combat and interface edges. Fortune Builder is interesting early construction and management play, with limited variety rather than the depth of later city builders.

## Release decisions

- Prefer ordinary English USA/shared USA-Europe releases. Use suitable named revisions within that preference, and do not add regional alternatives simply to collect another label.
- **Donkey Kong**, **Miner 2049er** and **Pitstop** use the shared USA/Europe **v1.1** releases. Their earlier USA or unversioned counterparts are omitted.
- **Evolution** uses the Canadian commercial release rather than the USA entry marked Unl. The Canadian BC games and Dam Busters are not added beside their selected USA counterparts.
- **Defender** uses the original USA entry. The separately labelled BUG Fixed version is held for an explicit compatibility/patch decision rather than automatically treated as a later retail revision.
- **Root Beer Tapper**, **H.E.R.O. - Helicopter Emergency Rescue Operation**, **Squish'em Featuring Sam** and the other games retain the DAT's exact naming. No unofficial renaming is implied by the shorter titles used in these notes.
- BIOS images, test cartridges, promotional material, betas, prototypes and unofficial variants are outside the selection. A complete-looking prototype is not silently promoted into the retail list.

## Deliberate overlap

The stronger arcade conversions remain worthwhile even when the same games appear on Atari or Sega lists. Lady Bug and Pepper II are especially characteristic choices; versions of Donkey Kong, Zaxxon, Gyruss and BurgerTime also help explain the platform's appeal.

Both Mr. Do! games, both BC adventures and both Frogger games remain as different experiences. Pitfall and Pitfall II likewise justify separate places. Q-bert's Qubes is a sequel with different rules, not a renamed copy of Q-bert.

H.E.R.O., Beamrider, River Raid and Keystone Kapers are retained as worthwhile interpretations rather than excluded for having familiar Activision names. Conversely, a licence alone does not earn a slot: Chuck Norris, Robin Hood and Tomarc are omitted as less persuasive uses of playing time.

The creative software and very young children's titles are not collected comprehensively. A selected arithmetic or picture puzzle is an individual gameplay choice, not a reason to include every learning cartridge.

## Keypads and special controllers

The standard ColecoVision controller includes a keypad and two side buttons. That keypad is part of the intended interface, not merely a way to pass the title screen. **Mouse Trap**, **Spy Hunter**, **Aquattack**, **Blockade Runner** and **WarGames** can need quick access to keypad actions. These remain deliberate selections, with comfortable bindings preferable to interrupting play for every command.

**Gateway to Apshai**, **Fortune Builder**, **The Dam Busters** and **2010** also deserve instructions and an understood keypad layout. Their inclusion accepts a learning and configuration requirement; it does not assume they behave like one-button arcade games. Optional Super Action support in Spy Hunter and Star Trek does not make those games exclusive to that accessory.

**Slither** and **Victory** are held for a deliberate Roller Controller mapping. **Turbo**, **Destructor** and **The Dukes of Hazzard** are held for the driving controller. **Rocky: Super Action Boxing** and the Super Action sports games likewise need an explicit control arrangement. These are control decisions rather than judgements that the games are unworthy.

The DAT has separate **Front Line Standard Controller** and **Super Action Controller** entries. Neither is selected automatically: choosing a more convenient filename is not a substitute for deciding which release and control arrangement to adopt. A controller adaptation can be considered explicitly later.

Centipede and Omega Race remain eligible through ordinary controller play despite their association with the Roller Controller. No special-controller hacks are silently substituted. Artillery Duel is held for a multiplayer collection rather than assumed to supply the same solo value as the selected games.

## Scope

This is the cartridge catalogue, not a general ADAM computer software collection. Modern homebrew and Super Game Module releases deserve a separate catalogue and selection rather than guessed filenames in this manifest. Unreleased projects such as Pac-Man, Dig Dug, Joust, Steamroller and Lord of the Dungeon are outside the default retail selection, regardless of their interest.

Original difficulty and quirks remain part of the games. Manuals and controller overlays are particularly useful for the adventures and simulations; the list does not silently replace them with patched or simplified editions.
