## Intent

A companion to the MSX cartridge collection, centred on the games that make MSX2 worth exploring: Space Manbow, Aleste, Vampire Killer, Metal Gear, Usas, Hinotori and the stronger arcade and puzzle releases. Keep worthwhile discoveries alongside the classics, while treating Japanese text and keyboard demands as real selection criteria.

This is the installed MSX2 cartridge DAT, not the whole MSX2 library. Aleste 2, Snatcher, SD Snatcher, Psycho World and many other important disk releases are outside its scope. No substitute filenames, disk conversions or fan translations are invented to fill those gaps.

Each selected game has one `.rom` file. The denominator includes alternative dumps, utilities, a BIOS entry and releases outside the selection; it is not a count of distinct retail games.

## What earns a place

| Strand | Examples | Why keep it |
|---|---|---|
| Defining games | Space Manbow, Aleste, Vampire Killer, Metal Gear, Treasure of Usas | Strong identities and substantial play. These are the main reasons to prepare an MSX2 setup. |
| Shooting | Zanac Ex, Scramble Formation, Darwin 4078, Andorogynus, Contra, Star Virgin | Different patterns, perspectives and movement systems rather than a single style repeated. |
| Arcade action | Bubble Bobble, Kiki Kaikai, Ninja-kun, Fantasy Zone II, Ikari, Xevious | Familiar ideas with worthwhile MSX interpretations. |
| Puzzle and competition | Arkanoid II, Quarth, Quinpl, Penguin-kun Wars 2, Tetris, King's Valley II | Approachable games for shorter sessions, with enough variety to keep them distinct. |
| Exploration and adventure | Hinotori, King Kong 2, Higemaru Makai Shima, Labyrinth, both Lupin games | More discovery and navigation, accepted with instructions or a guide where needed. |
| Smaller discoveries | Yuurei-kun, Monmon Kaibutsu, Girly Block, Rick to Mick, Deep Forest | Character and unusual mechanics beyond the most famous Konami names. |
| Sport | Dynamite Bowl, Family Billiards, Family Boxing, Hole in One Special, Pennant Race 2 | Changes of pace without filling the menu with every annual sports release. |
| Qualified curiosities | The Eidolon, Koronis Rift, Rastan Saga, Super Rambo Special, Zombie Hunter | Different experiences worth sampling, with greater patience or more modest expectations. |

The selection is intentionally broad, rather than claiming all 61 games are equally essential. Rastan is a qualified conversion, while The Eidolon and Koronis Rift offer unusual 3D experiences with a learning cost. Slower action adventures should not be judged as if they were immediate arcade games.

## Language and naming decisions

- **Metal Gear** uses the European English release. **Metal Gear 2 - Solid Snake** uses the Japanese original and is a deliberate language exception because of its importance. It requires Japanese comprehension or substantial guide support; a fan translation would be a separate derived build, not this verified ROM.
- **Treasure of Usas, The** and **Vampire Killer** use the ordinary Japan/Europe English releases. Do not also include their Virtual Console counterparts under different titles.
- **Ouke no Tani - El Giza no Fuuin** is the MSX2 version of **King's Valley II**. The contest-level collection is omitted. This complements the MSX1 selection, though the versions overlap substantially.
- **Yuurei-kun** is also known as **Mr. Ghost**; **Monmon Kaibutsu** as **Mon Mon Monster**. Keep the DAT names while making those familiar names discoverable here.
- The Japanese action games are not assumed to be entirely text-free. Hinotori, Goemon, Higemaru, King Kong 2 and the Lupin games are included as instruction- or guide-friendly imports. Their controls and objectives can require explanation.
- **Gekitotsu Pennant Race 2** uses the DAT's **English Menu** variant. This does not imply that every name or screen is translated.
- Dragon Quest, Hydlide 3, Wizardry, Ultima Exodus, Koei's historical strategy games and dialogue-led mysteries are held for their greater reading burden. Their reputation does not remove that obstacle.

## Release and overlap decisions

Four selected games use the official **Wii U Virtual Console** entries because the installed DAT does not offer the preferred ordinary Japanese release: Contra, Ganbare Goemon, Quarth and Space Manbow. Quarth's Korean entry is not substituted solely to avoid the rerelease tag. No unofficial patch is applied.

Use the ordinary release rather than Alt dumps elsewhere. No prototypes, demos, utilities, BIOS files or aftermarket games are selected. Modern homebrew could earn a place through a separate assessment; appearing in the DAT is not sufficient by itself.

**1942 is deliberately held here.** Both the MSX and MSX2 DATs name their entry `1942 (Japan).rom`, but the SHA-1 values differ:

- MSX: `0733cd627467a866846e15caf1770a5594eaf4cc`
- MSX2: `c9135b1e451193bd4c06d8143eaca934cc89fe05`

The existing MSX manifest already selects its version. Deploying both under their exact names into one folder would cause an overwrite, so the MSX2 selection avoids that collision. Choosing one version across the two lists, or providing distinct deployment names, would be a separate decision. The verified libraries can still retain both in their respective DAT folders.

Dragon Slayer IV and Topple Zip are also left to the existing MSX selection rather than repeating them automatically from this catalogue. The two manifests are complementary, not an instruction to collect every cross-generation variant.

## Controls and machine setup

Use an MSX2-capable emulator configuration and its required firmware. Cartridge sound hardware also matters: SCC and MSX-MUSIC support are part of the intended experience where used. A shared NextUI folder does not imply that every game should run in MSX1 mode.

Metal Gear and its sequel need inventory, radio and other keyboard actions in addition to movement and fire. Map the relevant keys rather than expecting a simple two-button layout. Exploration games and sports menus can also need occasional keyboard input. Vampire Killer's keys and room exploration distinguish it from simply replaying the NES Castlevania.

The flat manifest preserves verified game identities. It does not configure mapper detection, sound, firmware or per-game controls, and it does not claim the particular installed NextUI setup has been tested. Keep the MSX2 source list separate even when its deployment shares the MSX folder.
