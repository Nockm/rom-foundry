## Editorial approach

Discovery, worthwhile disk versions and genre variety, rather than an exhaustive historical archive. Familiar cartridge counterparts are not automatically excluded, but an ordinary disk conversion does not earn inclusion just by existing. Rarity and collector prices were not criteria.

Research supplied leads and counterarguments, supplemented by editorial nominations. Not every game has several independent endorsements, and this is not a claim of hands-on testing of every release. Some choices are deliberately rough-edged curiosities. The list is broader than a greatest-hits selection and has no fixed game quota.

| Area | Highlights and editorial intent |
|---|---|
| Exploration and action | Ai Senshi Nicol and Nazo no Murasamejou are central discoveries. Armana no Kiseki adds grappling-hook platforming; Meikyuu Jiin Dababa adds unusual tile-based movement. Fire Bam and Youkai Yashiki are further exploration picks. |
| Platforming | Bio Miracle Bokutte Upa, Doki Doki Panic and the Japanese Super Mario Bros. 2 provide substantial games with different identities. Wardner no Mori offers another arcade adaptation to explore. |
| Music and shooting | Otocky is a particularly distinctive music/shooter hybrid. Gall Force, Halley Wars and Reflect World widen the shooter pool. Gyruss and Zanac offer disk counterparts to familiar games. |
| Puzzles | Both Egger Land entries, both Kinetic Connection entries, Puzzle Boys and Namida no Soukoban Special provide several different forms of spatial problem-solving. Super Lode Runner and its sequel add puzzle/platforming; Clu Clu Land and Knight Move supply more immediate arcade puzzles. |
| Arcade and sport | Nazo no Kabe, Moonball Magic, Electrician, Smash Ping Pong and Putt Putt Golf add short-session alternatives. |
| Racing | Famicom Grand Prix: F1 Race, 3D Hot Rally and Vs. Excitebike supply three different racing styles. |

Falsion is a qualified technical curiosity, not an unreserved shooter recommendation: its perspective, visibility and difficulty can be frustrating. Armana's unusual grappling timing and Dababa's rigid movement also demand patience. Their distinctive ideas justify exploration without assuming polish throughout the collection.

## Release choices and aliases

- Exact DAT names are authoritative for copying. The manifest uses **Armana no Kiseki**, although articles also transliterate the title as Almana or Arumana. Likewise it uses **Egger Land**, **Final Command - Akai Yousai** and **Zanac (AI)** as catalogued.
- Select one ordinary commercial release per game group, including official Disk Writer distributions. A `(Disk Writer)` label is not a prototype label.
- Where appropriate, pin the DAT's numbered revisions and selected Disk Writer versions: Akumajou Dracula Rev 2, Metroid Rev 3, Hikari Shinwa Rev 1 and Knight Move Rev 1. F1 Race uses DV 1, 3D Hot Rally DV 8, and Doki Doki Panic and Super Mario Bros. 2 use DV 2. These are explicit image choices, not a general assertion that all DV labels form a chronological upgrade scale.
- Do not swap in Virtual Console reissues, prize disks, alternate revisions or whatever image happens to be available during deployment. Change the manifest deliberately if a different verified release becomes preferable.
- **All Night Nippon Super Mario Bros.** is an explicit promotional-release exception. Its altered Mario presentation earns a small historical-curiosity place, not a claim that it is a whole new full-length Mario adventure. Its rarity is not the reason for inclusion.
- BIOS images, developer disks, prototypes, betas, pirate variants and aftermarket software are excluded. No fan translation is silently substituted for a DAT entry.

## Overlap with NES and other lists

The existing NES manifest is unchanged. These overlaps are deliberate:

- **Akumajou Dracula, Metroid and Hikari Shinwa** retain original disk experiences alongside Castlevania, Metroid and Kid Icarus on NES. Disk saving and version-specific presentation are reasons to compare them; this is not a claim that every disk port has better audio or is preferable in every respect.
- **Bio Miracle Bokutte Upa** uses the original disk release here even if a cartridge counterpart is selected elsewhere.
- **Doki Doki Panic** overlaps substantially with the Western Super Mario Bros. 2, but its original characters, presentation and progression make it worth retaining as an intentional comparison.
- **Super Mario Bros. 2** here is the Japanese sequel known internationally as The Lost Levels, not the Western game derived from Doki Doki Panic.
- **Vs. Excitebike** adds a distinct expanded disk version alongside the original Excitebike. The FDS version is also distinct from the arcade VS. release.
- **Final Command** is the disk relative of Jackal; **Nazo no Kabe** is the relative of Crackout. **Gyruss** and **Zanac** are also intentional version comparisons. They are not counted as globally deduplicated games across platforms.
- The **Egger Land** games remain eligible alongside Adventures of Lolo. Shared series mechanics and some familiar puzzles do not make every instalment interchangeable.

Ordinary disk copies of Super Mario Bros., Donkey Kong, Pac-Man, Bubble Bobble and similar familiar games are not added merely to reproduce the NES list.

## Language and practical holds

All selections are Japanese-market images. Explicit `(En)` tags are retained where the DAT provides them; an absent English tag is not proof that the game is text-heavy. Ai Senshi Nicol is a particularly approachable import whose in-game text is English, as discussed in the linked individual review. Most selections foreground action, racing or puzzles, but Japanese menus and instructions can still require a guide. Learning the rules is especially relevant for the Kinetic Connection games, Knight Move, Reflect World and the less familiar arcade choices.

Text-heavy games are held for an explicit language decision: **Esper Dream**, the **Deep Dungeon** games, **Famicom Tantei Club**, **Shin Onigashima**, **Yuuyuuki**, **Jinguji Saburou**, **Dead Zone** and **Suishou no Dragon**. Historical importance alone does not make an untranslated adventure a good default for this English-first collection.

The Japanese **Zelda no Densetsu**, **Link no Bouken** and **Dracula II** are also deferred here: the existing NES list supplies English versions, while the original disk releases need a more deliberate language/comparison decision. This is not a judgement against the games. Dandy, Silviana and other RPG-like discoveries can be revisited with the same language policy.

Keyboard/music tools, quizzes, specialist peripherals and communication or promotional utilities are outside the gameplay selection. Doremikko in particular is not treated as an ordinary gamepad game. Falsion and 3D Hot Rally need review in their ordinary non-stereoscopic modes; shutter glasses and stereoscopic output are not assumed.

## Research inputs

Individual reviews inform both nominations and caveats; broad lists were used for discovery rather than copied as a ranking.

- [Hardcore Gaming 101: Ai Senshi Nicol](https://www.hardcoregaming101.net/ai-senshi-nicol/) — action/exploration and language accessibility.
- [Hardcore Gaming 101: Otocky](https://www.hardcoregaming101.net/otocky-2/) — the musical interaction that makes this selection distinctive.
- [Hardcore Gaming 101: Almana no Kiseki](https://www.hardcoregaming101.net/almana-no-kiseki/) — grappling mechanics and their frustrations.
- [Hardcore Gaming 101: Meikyuu Jiin Dababa](https://www.hardcoregaming101.net/meikyuu-jiin-dababa/) — movement, puzzles and difficulty caveats.
- [Hardcore Gaming 101: Falsion](https://www.hardcoregaming101.net/falsion/) — a critical counterweight to technical-showcase recommendations.
- [Nintendo Life: Eight NES games on Famicom Disk System](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2021/02/feature_8_nes_games_that_were_better_on_famicom_disk_system) — leads for meaningful version comparisons, without adopting a blanket superiority claim.
- [Nintendo Life: Vs. Excitebike review](https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/wiiu-eshop/vs_excitebike_nes) — the disk game's extra modes and limitations.
- [Pimp My Retro Handheld: FDS selection](https://www.pimyretro.org/game-selections/games-selection-famicom-disk-system/) — a wider candidate pool; its translation suggestions are not adopted automatically.
