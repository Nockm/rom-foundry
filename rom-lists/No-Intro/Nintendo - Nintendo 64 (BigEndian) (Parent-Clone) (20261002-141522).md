## Editorial approach

A broad collection for exploration, rather than a short greatest-hits list. The obvious Nintendo and Rare games sit alongside worthwhile sports, licensed adaptations, arcade ports and unusual experiments. There is no fixed quota and no requirement to fill every genre. Not every inclusion is equally essential.

Research supplied recommendation pools and individual perspectives, supplemented by editorial nominations. This is not a claim of hands-on testing of every title, several independent endorsements for every selection, or tested compatibility with a particular handheld. Inclusion reflects quality, originality, historical interest or a worthwhile kind of play; rough edges are allowed when the attraction is clear.

| Area | Examples and selection intent |
|---|---|
| Platforming and adventure | Super Mario 64, both Banjo games, both Zeldas, Paper Mario, Conker and Rayman 2 anchor the collection. Rocket: Robot on Wheels, SpaceStation Silicon Valley, both Goemon games and Mischief Makers add distinctive alternatives. |
| Action and exploration | Blast Corps, Body Harvest, Jet Force Gemini, Shadow Man and WinBack provide very different approaches. Hybrid Heaven and Shadowgate 64 are more qualified discoveries, selected for unusual ideas rather than universal acclaim. |
| Shooters | GoldenEye, Perfect Dark, Doom 64, the Turok campaigns, both Duke Nukem interpretations, Forsaken and the N64 Quake games cover several styles. Star Fox, the Star Wars flight games, Sin and Punishment, Bangai-O and Star Soldier broaden the perspective. |
| Racing | Mario Kart, Diddy Kong Racing, F-Zero X, Wave Race, Beetle Adventure Racing, Excitebike 64 and the Rush games are joined by rally, circuit, stunt and combat racers. World Driver Championship, Stunt Racer 64, Road Rash 64 and Re-Volt are worthwhile discoveries. |
| Sports | Mario Golf and Tennis, 1080, both Snowboard Kids games, Tony Hawk, selected baseball/basketball/football/hockey games and International Track & Field give both arcade and simulation options. Entire annual franchises are not collected by default. |
| Fighting and wrestling | Smash Bros., Killer Instinct Gold, Fighters Destiny, Mace, Rakugakids and Mortal Kombat offer varied combat. WCW-nWo Revenge, WrestleMania 2000, No Mercy and Virtual Pro Wrestling 2 represent worthwhile AKI variations. |
| Puzzles and strategy | Pokemon Puzzle League, The New Tetris, Tetrisphere, Wetrix, Magical Tetris Challenge and further arcade puzzlers provide short-session play. Ogre Battle 64, StarCraft, Command & Conquer, Worms and Virtual Chess give longer or more methodical options. |
| Party and collections | All three Mario Party games, several Bomberman interpretations, Gauntlet Legends and Pokemon Stadium are deliberately retained despite their multiplayer appeal. Pokemon Snap remains a distinctive single-player choice. |

Licensed discoveries include Toy Story 2, Donald Duck, Spider-Man, Duck Dodgers and Indiana Jones. A licence alone neither earns nor rules out a place. Some adaptations are less polished than their counterparts on other platforms, but this collection is not globally deduplicated across systems.

## DAT scope and release policy

- Use the **BigEndian** DAT and its `.z64` ROM names. The ByteSwapped DAT describes an alternative representation and should not create duplicate copies in the same deployment. Renaming an extension does not convert byte order.
- The separate **Nintendo 64DD** and **Mario no Photopi SmartMedia** DATs are outside this cartridge selection. No 64DD disk images, cartridge conversions of disk software or accessory data are implied.
- Prefer an ordinary English USA cartridge release and the highest numbered revision catalogued within that region. English-containing USA language sets are retained exactly. 1080 uses the shared Japan/USA English/Japanese release.
- European releases supply Rakugakids and F-1 World Grand Prix II, which do not have an ordinary USA counterpart in this DAT. The European release's timing/display behaviour is not assumed identical to a USA game.
- These are pinned choices, not availability-based fallbacks. Revisions such as Ocarina of Time Rev 2, WWF No Mercy Rev 1, Pokemon Stadium Rev 2 and Turok: Rage Wars Rev 1 remain explicit filenames.
- Ordinary cartridge releases take precedence over GameCube, Virtual Console, LodgeNet and modern publisher reissues. The list does not silently prefer a later reissue just because its revision number is larger.
- Prototypes, betas, demonstrations, test cartridges, cheat utilities, pirate releases, unofficial hacks, fan translations and aftermarket games are excluded.
- Ocarina of Time uses the ordinary cartridge game. The separate GameCube Master Quest release is held for a later deliberate variant decision.

## Deliberate overlap and representatives

**Castlevania: Legacy of Darkness** represents the N64 Castlevania branch here; the original Castlevania is omitted as substantial overlap. This is an editorial space-saving choice, not a claim that every detail of the original routes is identical.

All three **Mario Party** games remain for their distinct boards and minigame pools. Both **Pokemon Stadium** games remain for their different content, and both **Snowboard Kids** games and the three **Tony Hawk** entries remain as worthwhile sequels. Returning mechanics do not automatically make sequels redundant.

**International Superstar Soccer '98** and **2000** are intentionally retained as different football options, alongside FIFA 98 and 99. Madden NFL 2001, All-Star Baseball 2001, NHL 99 and NBA Courtside 2 represent their respective simulation branches; there is no attempt to retain every annual roster. NBA Hangtime and Showtime, NFL Blitz 2000 and Wayne Gretzky's 3D Hockey provide separate arcade alternatives.

**Bomberman 64 (USA)** is the 3D adventure; **Bomberman 64 (Japan)** is the different, later 2D game. They are not two regional copies of the same game, and both are deliberately selected alongside The Second Attack! and Bomberman Hero. The Japanese original equivalent to the USA adventure is not separately selected.

Ready 2 Rumble and Round 2, the three Rush games, both BattleTanx games and the selected Turok entries offer enough differences to retain in a broad collection. Turok: Rage Wars is especially oriented toward arena play rather than another equivalent campaign. Later tightening can remove such alternatives without revising the underlying verified library.

## Imports and language holds

| Selected Japanese import | Accessibility consideration |
|---|---|
| Bakuretsu Muteki Bangaioh | Bangai-O's N64 version. Action is the main draw; Japanese dialogue and presentation remain. Its tiny sprites and directional shooting deserve a handheld trial. |
| Tsumi to Batsu: Hoshi no Keishousha | Sin and Punishment. The manifest uses the original Japanese cartridge image, not an assumed translated Virtual Console version. Menu/tutorial guidance may be needed. |
| Bomberman 64 | The later 2D game, with Japanese menus and minigame navigation. Distinct from the Western 3D adventure. |
| Puyo Puyo Sun 64 | Match play is the attraction; Japanese story and menus remain. |
| Puyo Puyoon Party | A further puzzle variation; Japanese text and rules may need outside instructions. |
| Virtual Pro Wrestling 2: Oudou Keishou | A deliberate specialist exception with a substantial Japanese interface. Worth exploring for AKI wrestling enthusiasts, but a menu/control guide is recommended. It is not an English-ready default. |

Tetris 64 is also a Japanese-region selection, but the DAT explicitly tags it English. This list does not depend on a Bio Sensor setup or promise its accessory features.

Custom Robo and Custom Robo V2, Wonder Project J2, Shiren 2 and Doubutsu no Mori are held for an explicit language/translation decision. Densha de Go! 64 and Dezaemon 3D likewise need a more deliberate assessment of instructions, controls and utility features. They are not replaced with unverified patches.

Hey You, Pikachu! is held for microphone/peripheral review. Mario no Photopi is not treated as an ordinary gamepad game. Superman, notoriously weak licensed adaptations and poor fighters are not included merely to cover their franchises; rarity likewise does not secure Clay Fighter: Sculptor's Cut a place.

## Distinctive play requirements

N64 games often rely on precise analogue movement and C-button camera or aiming controls. That matters particularly for shooters, flight games and Sin and Punishment. Performance-heavy games remain eligible on their own merits rather than being curated around one handheld.

Pokemon Stadium is selected for rental play and minigames as well as its wider appeal; imported teams and Transfer Pak features are not prerequisites. Mario Golf and Tennis are likewise worthwhile without their cross-platform extras. Multiplayer-focused games remain eligible for their intended social play, without assuming a single handheld reproduces that experience.

Conker, the violent shooters and horror games are adult-oriented selections.
