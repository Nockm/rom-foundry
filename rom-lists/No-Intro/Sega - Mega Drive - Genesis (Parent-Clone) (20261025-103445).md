## Approach

Broad quality and discovery rather than just a best-of list. Familiar classics sit alongside lesser-known action games, genre specialists, worthwhile licensed games, sports and historical curiosities. This is editorial judgement informed by the sources below, not a claim that every title has multiple independent endorsements or has been play-tested here. Breadth and worthwhile variety matter more than a fixed game count.

Coverage includes platformers, cinematic adventures, beat-'em-ups, fighting games, scrolling shooters, action RPGs, tactical games, strategy, puzzles, pinball, racing and sports. Sports picks favour distinct play styles and representative seasons rather than every annual release. Some challenging or uneven games earn a place for their mechanics or historical interest; inclusion is not a guarantee of universal appeal.

Examples of discovery choices include Atomic Runner, Twinkle Tale, Granada, Sub-Terrania, Puggsy, Bubba 'N' Stix, Light Crusader, Master of Monsters, Starflight, The Ooze and Haunting Starring Polterguy. These examples are editorial nominations rather than quotations from a source.

## Release decisions

- Prefer ordinary retail releases over betas, prototypes, samples and modern reissue variants. Prefer suitable English USA releases, then World and Europe. Retain European editions where appropriate.
- Shining Force, Phantasy Star and other text-heavy selections use English releases rather than untranslated Japanese editions.
- Monster World IV is an explicit exception to the ordinary-retail preference: select the officially localised English Virtual Console ROM catalogued by No-Intro, not the untranslated Japanese cartridge. No fan patch was silently selected.
- Japanese action/puzzle imports are included where text is not the central gameplay barrier: Pulseman, Advanced Busterhawk Gley Lancer, Undead Line, Verytex, Battle Mania Daiginjou, Twinkle Tale, Puyo Puyo Tsuu and Yu Yu Hakusho: Makyou Toitsusen. Menus and story may still be Japanese. Original Battle Mania is represented by its Western release, Trouble Shooter.
- Sonic 3 and Sonic & Knuckles are retained individually and as Sonic 3 & Knuckles. This is a deliberate play-mode exception to avoiding redundant releases. Generic multicarts and duplicate regional releases are excluded. Sonic 2 uses the ordinary World Rev A entry rather than a compilation variant.
- Lightening Force represents Thunder Force IV; do not add the European title as another game. Other regional naming pairs likewise have a single representative (for example Beyond Oasis and The Story of Thor).
- Langrisser II, The Hybrid Front, Surging Aura and other text-heavy untranslated imports are excluded pending an explicit translation policy. Modern commercial homebrew and aftermarket games can be considered separately. Sega CD, 32X and Master System games are outside this list.
- Controls and performance on the actual handheld still need practical testing. Six-button fighters, J-Cart games and Virtua Racing merit particular attention. No assumption of linked or multiplayer-only functionality is required for inclusion.
