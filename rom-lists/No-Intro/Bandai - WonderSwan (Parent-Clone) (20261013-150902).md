## Intent

A monochrome companion to the WonderSwan Color collection. Start with Klonoa, Buffers Evolution, Makaimura and Pocket Fighter, then explore the machine's particularly useful supply of puzzle games. Keep a little rhythm, sport and simulation for variety without treating every Japanese licence as an automatic inclusion.

The DAT includes a large amount of WonderWitch software, development tools, revisions and a boot ROM. Its 257 entries are not 257 different commercial games. Each of the 33 selected games has one ROM file.

## What earns a place

| Strand | Games and examples | Why keep them |
|---|---|---|
| Platform and action anchors | Kaze no Klonoa - Moonlight Museum; Buffers Evolution; Makaimura | Distinctive games worth visiting this machine for, rather than filling the menu with ports alone. |
| Arcade action | Pocket Fighter; Crazy Climber; Ganso Jajamaru-kun; Space Invaders | Familiar play with different movement and control demands. |
| Puzzle staples | GunPey; Magical Drop; Puyo Puyo Tsuu; Puzzle Bobble | Immediate rules with room to improve. Their similarity to games elsewhere does not make them interchangeable. |
| Logic and board puzzles | Slither Link; Ou-chan no Oekaki Logic; Glocal Hexcite; Shanghai Pocket; Gomoku Narabe & Reversi | Slower sessions and visual reasoning, with limited reliance on following a Japanese story. |
| Puzzle discoveries | Engacho!; Mingle Magnet; Sennou Millennium; Tane o Maku Tori | Different ideas that reward a short explanation of their rules. |
| Exploration and familiar variants | Lode Runner; Rainbow Islands - Putty's Party | Further platform-puzzle play, with qualified expectations of these particular versions. |
| Rhythm and music | beatmania; Turntablist - DJ Battle | A change of pace, accepted with more demanding input and timing requirements. |
| Sport | Fire Pro Wrestling; Nice On; Side Pocket; Soccer Yarou!; Wonder Stadium '99 | Wrestling, golf, pool, football and baseball without collecting every sports release. |
| Qualified discoveries | Armored Unit; Densha de Go! 2; English Digimon | Tactical play, train driving and virtual-pet management broaden the selection for someone willing to learn their systems. |

This is a generous selection, not a claim that every game is essential. Rainbow Islands - Putty's Party is a qualified variant, not a substitute for the arcade original. Makaimura is worth including but remains demanding. Soccer Yarou!, Nice On and Wonder Stadium '99 earn room as changes of pace rather than defining classics.

Engacho!'s comic gross-out presentation is part of its identity. It is selected for its puzzle mechanics rather than being mistaken for a conventional falling-block game.

## Release and overlap decisions

- **Kaze no Klonoa - Moonlight Museum** belongs here, not in the Color DAT. Keep the Japanese original; a fan translation would be a separately managed derived release.
- **Makaimura for WonderSwan** is a distinct entry in the Ghosts 'n Goblins series. It is not simply the SuperGrafx Daimakaimura selected elsewhere.
- **Digimon - Ver. WonderSwan** uses the official Hong Kong English release. This virtual-pet game is distinct from the English Anode/Cathode Tamer adventure in the Color list.
- Fire Pro Wrestling uses Rev 5; Mingle Magnet and Nice On use Rev 1. The other selected games use their ordinary full releases.
- Keep the original **GunPey** as a defining monochrome game alongside Color's GunPey EX. Omit **Tare Panda no GunPey** rather than adding another themed treatment automatically.
- Keep **Final Lap 2000** as the monochrome racing entry, while the separate Color list retains Final Lap Special. These are separate releases, not regional duplicates.
- **Wonder Stadium '99** represents that series here. The earlier Wonder Stadium is omitted.
- **Densha de Go! 2** represents the train games, without automatically adding the first release as well.
- No prototypes, demos, utilities, BIOS files or WonderWitch submissions are selected. They require their own assessment rather than inheriting a recommendation from catalogue membership.

## Language and control considerations

Japanese menus and occasional story text remain. Klonoa's platform puzzles and the arcade games are more approachable without translation than the machine's narrative RPGs. Slither Link and Ou-chan's picture logic become accessible after learning their interfaces; the latter is a nonogram game, not a language quiz.

Armored Unit is a qualified, guide-friendly tactics inclusion. Densha de Go! 2 needs an explanation of its instruments, braking and stopping requirements. The English Digimon release avoids a translation barrier, but raising a creature and learning its routines is a different commitment from playing an arcade level.

Crazy Climber uses both directional clusters to control the climber's arms independently. Keep it as an interesting use of the original hardware, but provide a deliberate mapping rather than assuming a single D-pad is enough. Rhythm games similarly need comfortable access to several inputs and responsive audio timing. Rotation and control orientation should be considered together.

The NextUI WSC pak supports both `.ws` and `.wsc` games in **Bandai WonderSwan Color (WSC)**. The two DAT manifests can share that deployment folder while remaining separate in the verified library. The manifest does not install the emulator pak or configure per-game controls.

## Deliberate holds

Clock Tower, the Japanese Digimon adventures, Chocobo's dungeon game, Langrisser, Vaitz Blade, Super Robot Taisen, the strategy-heavy Gundam titles and the dialogue-led mysteries are held for their reading burden. A strong reputation does not eliminate that obstacle, and the list does not silently substitute translations.

Rockman & Forte is held rather than included solely for its licence. The Color list's Rockman EXE WS already provides a more inviting Mega Man action starting point. Board games requiring substantial rules knowledge, language quizzes, pachinko and horse-racing prediction software are also outside this selection.

Robot Works is held for separate review of its standalone play and WonderBorg accessory functions. Handy Sonar depends on its fishing accessory; Tenori-on is a music tool rather than an automatic game inclusion. These decisions keep unusual hardware from becoming an unexplained deployment requirement.
