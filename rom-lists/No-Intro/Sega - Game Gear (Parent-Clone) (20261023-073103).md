## Approach

A broad collection built around the handheld's own strengths, with worthwhile ports and discoveries alongside the obvious favourites. The GG Shinobi games, Aleste shooters, Tails Adventure, Magical Puzzle Popils and Defenders of Oasis provide reasons to explore Game Gear even when Master System and Mega Drive are already represented.

Quality, distinctive ideas and genre variety matter more than rarity or a fixed game count. This is deliberately more generous than a short essentials list. Inclusion is editorial judgement informed by reviews, not a claim that every game has been personally play-tested or received unanimous praise.

| Area | Selection intent |
|---|---|
| Platforming and action | Both GG Shinobi games, Ristar, the Mickey and Donald adventures, Psychic World and Dynamite Headdy anchor the selection. Tempo Jr., Coca-Cola Kid, Donald no Magical World and licensed adventures provide lighter discoveries. |
| Shooting | GG Aleste, Power Strike II and GG Aleste 3 lead a smaller but strong selection. Halley Wars, Griffin, Aerial Assault, Dropzone and the distinctive Fantasy Zone interpretation broaden it. Gunstar Heroes adds ambitious run-and-gun action. |
| Puzzles and arcade play | Magical Puzzle Popils, Baku Baku, Putt & Putter, Junction, Shikinjou, Kinetic Connection and Gear Works offer different kinds of problem-solving. Columns, Super Columns, Bust-A-Move and Mean Bean Machine cover familiar falling-piece play. |
| Adventures and tactics | Tails Adventure supplies exploration and gadgets. Defenders of Oasis, Shining Force: The Sword of Hajya, Crystal Warriors and Dragon Crystal offer different longer-form experiences in English. The Dizzy games add inventory puzzles. |
| Racing, sports and fighting | Micro Machines I/II, GP Rider, OutRun, Power Drive and Road Rash cover different driving styles. Representative football, golf, tennis, baseball and hockey choices sit alongside Fatal Fury Special, Samurai Shodown, Mortal Kombat II and the Power Rangers games. |

Not every inclusion is an essential. Ax Battler is a qualified adventure/action curiosity; Crystal Warriors has interesting elemental tactics but awkward commands and sparse storytelling. Mega Man is an unusual handheld adaptation with visibility compromises. Tails' Skypatrol has a steep learning curve, and Sonic 2 can be frustrating within the smaller view. These remain worth exploring without pretending their faults disappear.

## Release decisions

- Prefer ordinary English USA/shared Western releases, then suitable World or European releases. Japanese releases with explicit English DAT tags remain eligible. Regional counterparts are not collected merely for different titles.
- **Sonic the Hedgehog** uses World Rev A. **Shinobi** uses World Rev A, **Aerial Assault** World Rev 1, **Psychic World** the Western Rev 1, **Star Wars** Rev 1, and **Shanghai II** Japanese English-tagged Rev A. Prototypes and entries labelled Putative Proto are not substitutes for retail revisions.
- **Power Strike II** represents GG Aleste II. It is distinct from the Master System game called Power Strike II. The original Japanese English-tagged **GG Aleste** is also retained.
- **GG Aleste 3** is an explicit later-release exception: the official M2 game from Aleste Collection, not a prototype or fan sequel. Its collection-tagged DAT entry is selected because this is a new game, rather than another copy of an existing selection.
- **The Sword of Hajya** represents Shining Force Gaiden II in English. **Crystal Warriors** represents Arliel, **Defenders of Oasis** Shadam Crusader, **Factory Panic** Ganbare Gorby!, **Galaga 2** Galaga '91, and **Slider** Skweek.
- **Mean Bean Machine** represents the first Puyo Puyo's underlying play; its Japanese counterpart is omitted. **Puyo Puyo Tsuu** remains as a substantial sequel. **Sonic Chaos** represents Sonic & Tails, and **Triple Trouble** Sonic & Tails 2.
- BIOS files, betas, demos, sample cartridges, pirate releases, unofficial hacks and fan translations are outside the selection. Routine Virtual Console and compilation duplicates are omitted.

## Overlap and cartridge formats

The Game Gear Shinobi games are different from the Master System arcade adaptation. Tails Adventure is an exploration game rather than another conventional Sonic platformer; Tails' Skypatrol supplies a separate flying challenge. Triple Trouble earns its own place alongside Sonic 1, Sonic 2 and Chaos. Sonic Drift 2 represents the racing spin-offs; Sonic Drift, Sonic Blast and Sonic Labyrinth are omitted as less compelling alternatives.

The Game Gear versions of Gunstar Heroes, Dynamite Headdy, Ristar and the Streets of Rage games remain eligible for their handheld interpretations. Their presence is not a claim that each is preferable to its Mega Drive counterpart. Likewise, shared Mickey, Donald and Wonder Boy games are retained without automatic cross-system deduplication.

Seven selected cartridges use **Master System compatibility mode**, and this DAT correctly gives their ROMs `.sms` filenames: Castle of Illusion, Chase H.Q., The Excellent Dizzy Collection, Fantastic Dizzy, Olympic Gold, OutRun Europa and Prince of Persia. They belong to this Game Gear catalogue and remain in its manifest with those exact extensions. Their eventual deployment needs to preserve the appropriate mode rather than renaming them to `.gg`.

The Excellent Dizzy Collection is retained for its smaller companion games alongside the separate Fantastic Dizzy adventure. Collections are considered for distinct playable content rather than included automatically.

## Imports and holds

Gunstar Heroes, Berlin no Kabe, Magical Taruruuto-kun and Coca-Cola Kid are selected for action or puzzle play despite Japanese presentation. Buster Ball and Dodge Danpei add unusual sports, but their rules and menus may need outside instructions. The two Puzzle & Action collections likewise require accepting Japanese minigame prompts and consulting a guide where necessary. Their inclusion does not imply that every screen is language-free.

English-tagged imports such as Griffin, Pop Breaker, Kinetic Connection, Shikinjou and Yu Yu Hakusho are ordinary DAT releases, not patched translations. Donald no Magical World and Tails Adventure explicitly include English/Japanese language tags.

Royal Stone, Sylvan Tale, the other Shining Force Gaiden games, Phantasy Star Adventure/Gaiden, Lunar: Sanposuru Gakuen, Moldorian, Eternal Legend and the Madou Monogatari RPGs are held for an explicit language or translation decision. They are not dismissed as poor games. The Japanese Nazo Puyo puzzle challenges also need a deliberate decision about reading their objectives.

Mahjong, quizzes, pet simulations and casino variants are not collected comprehensively. Annual sports releases are represented selectively. Panzer Dragoon Mini, Rise of the Robots, Batman Forever and Journey from Darkness: Strider Returns do not earn places merely for their names or historical interest.
