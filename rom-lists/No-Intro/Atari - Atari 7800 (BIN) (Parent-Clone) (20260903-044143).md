## Approach

A generous introduction to a compact commercial library, supplemented by a small selection of completed community games. Strong arcade conversions are the foundation, while Food Fight, Ninja Golf, Midnight Mutants, Ballblazer and Tower Toppler help give the collection an identity beyond improved versions of 2600 games.

There is no fixed quota or attempt to collect every release. The reduction statistic includes regional versions, prototypes and later projects in the DAT, rather than counting only distinct original commercial games. Inclusion reflects editorial judgement informed by research, not a claim of hands-on testing or unanimous acclaim.

| Area | Selection intent |
|---|---|
| Arcade action | Food Fight, Robotron, Centipede, Joust, Dig Dug, Galaga, Xevious and Ms. Pac-Man form a strong core. Both Donkey Kong games, Mario Bros., Asteroids and Choplifter add different kinds of familiar arcade play. |
| Shooting and combat | Commando, Ikari Warriors, Xenophobe and Desert Falcon offer contrasting combat styles. Alien Brigade and Crossbow remain eligible through their ordinary joystick options. |
| Exploration and platforming | Midnight Mutants, Tower Toppler, Scrapyard Dog and Impossible Mission supply longer challenges. Dark Chambers offers simpler dungeon exploration; Ninja Golf mixes an unusual sporting premise with fighting. |
| Sports and vehicles | Ballblazer, Mean 18 Ultimate Golf, Hat Trick, One-on-One Basketball and Pete Rose Baseball cover different sports. Pole Position II, Fatal Run, Motor Psycho and Super Skateboardin' provide different approaches to movement and racing. |
| Discoveries | Crack'ed and Jinks offer less familiar arcade ideas. Basketbrawl and Fight Night add alternative sporting combat, while F-18 Hornet and Super Huey are qualified flight-simulation curiosities. |

This is broader than an essentials list. Midnight Mutants has appealing exploration but an unforgiving structure and benefits from its map. Planet Smashers, Motor Psycho and the flight simulations are more qualified discoveries than reasons to buy the system. Summer Games and Winter Games provide event-based variety without a claim that these are the strongest versions across all platforms.

## Release decisions

- Prefer ordinary USA/NTSC commercial releases. European alternatives are not included merely to collect another region, and betas or prototypes do not replace the selected retail games.
- **Impossible Mission** deliberately uses **Europe**. The original USA release has a puzzle-placement bug that can prevent completion; the PAL conversion fixed it. The USA Rev 1 entry is marked as a prototype and is not silently substituted.
- **Fatal Run** uses the USA 7800 release. Its selection is separate from the release history of the 2600 game; the two catalogues are not interchangeable.
- **Pete Rose Baseball** represents the corresponding European release titled Baseball. **Robotron - 2084**, **Choplifter!** and the other titles retain their exact DAT spelling and punctuation.
- Original Ms. Pac-Man and Asteroids are retained alongside selected later adaptations for their role in the commercial library. Later editions do not silently replace the originals.
- Development and diagnostic cartridges, BIOS images, incomplete games, generic multicarts and alternate regional builds are not playable selections.

## Completed community additions

The original library is small enough that a few carefully chosen later games make a substantial difference. Seven releases are explicit exceptions to the original-commercial focus:

| Game | Reason and selected release |
|---|---|
| Bentley Bear's Crystal Quest | A substantial platform adventure expanding the Crystal Castles character's world. Select the DAT's NTSC release; its later Atari publication does not imply that every cartridge edition has identical ROM bytes. |
| Asteroids Deluxe | Additional arcade mechanics give it a separate place beside Asteroids. Select NTSC. |
| Dungeon Stalker | Maze combat, limited ammunition and successive dungeon challenges add a distinctive score-chasing game. Select the DAT's 1.245d release. |
| Frenzy | Adds both Frenzy and Berzerk, including their different wall behaviour. Select v1.07 rather than retaining v1.06 as well. |
| Pac-Man Collection | A representative collection of Pac-Man variations beyond the original standalone Ms. Pac-Man. Select the original Digital NTSC entry, with Remastered, Ultimate and 40th Anniversary editions held as overlapping alternatives. |
| Scramble | A worthwhile horizontal arcade shooter. Select NTSC v1.15 rather than the older v1.14. |
| Super Pac-Man | Different maze rules make this more than another regional Pac-Man copy. Select NTSC v1.10 rather than v1.09. |

Their Aftermarket/Unl labels are retained exactly. These are deliberate completed-game choices, not an invitation to include every homebrew demo or unfinished experiment. Optional speech and score-saving accessories are not assumed to be available.

## Controls and omissions

**Alien Brigade** and **Crossbow** both support a joystick, so their light-gun origins do not exclude them. Barnyard Blaster, Meltdown and Sentinel are held for a deliberate light-gun arrangement. Aiming with a pad is a compromise, not a claim to reproduce a gun's feel.

**Robotron** deserves a comfortable control arrangement for movement and independent firing. The original 7800 supports two joysticks, and emulator mappings can differ. The list retains the game for its quality without assuming that the handheld supplies an arcade twin-stick setup.

Two-button controls are part of the platform's design. Ninja Golf, the platformers and other action games should retain their intended separate actions rather than being treated as one-button 2600 games. Flight simulations and adventures may need manuals; original design friction is not silently removed with patches.

Karateka, Mat Mania Challenge and Double Dragon are omitted as less persuasive uses of playing time. Tank Command and Water Ski are not included merely for their rarity. Further baseball, football and wrestling releases are held in favour of the selected representatives. Backward-compatible 2600 software belongs to its own manifest and is not duplicated here.

## Cartridge format

The manifest follows the **BIN DAT**, which contains the original commercial cartridge catalogue in raw `.bin` form. The installed A78 DAT has a different set of entries and cannot stand in for it simply because A78 is a familiar emulator extension.

ProSystem documents both `.bin` and `.a78` input. Preserve these canonical filenames in the verified library and point any deployment mapping at the BIN catalogue. A headered deployment, if chosen, is a separate preparation step with appropriate cartridge metadata; changing the extension alone does not add a header. Selection does not establish compatibility of every later cartridge design with the installed emulator.
